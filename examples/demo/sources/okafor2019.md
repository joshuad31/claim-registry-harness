# SYNTHETIC — Multi-site field trial of foliar silicate in rainfed wheat (okafor2019)

> Fabricated demonstration source. Not a real study. Do not cite as evidence.

## Abstract

Foliar silicate was tested at three rainfed field sites over two seasons to
determine whether it improves wheat grain yield under water-limited conditions.

## Methods

The trial used a randomized complete block design at three sites with four
replicates per treatment. Foliar potassium silicate was applied at 2 mL per litre
at tillering and at booting. The control received a water spray. Plots were 12
square metres. Grain yield was the primary outcome. Sites differed in seasonal
rainfall, from 210 to 340 millimetres.

## Results

Across the three sites, grain yield did not differ significantly between silicate
and control plots. Mean yield was 3.42 tonnes per hectare in treated plots and
3.51 tonnes per hectare in controls. The difference was not significant at any
individual site. Stomatal conductance was higher in treated plots at the driest
site, by 0.04 mol per square metre per second, but the difference did not
translate into a yield difference. Grain protein content was similar between
treatments.

## Discussion

The absence of a yield response in the field contrasts with positive greenhouse
reports. Possible reasons include lower effective leaf coverage in a field canopy,
rain washing the foliar film, and milder drought than in pot experiments.

## Limitations

Only two seasons were observed and the driest site received supplementary
irrigation twice. The trial cannot rule out a small yield effect below its
detection limit.
