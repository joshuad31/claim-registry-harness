## Stage B — ACH assessment

### B1. Preserve the question; expose its necessary conditions

In every **full** fragment, include the complete supplied questions and hypothesis wording (including qualifications) **verbatim**. Preserve earlier frames as history when the user changes them. Never silently broaden H3 or strengthen H1’s requirements. If definitions were never supplied, infer:

- **H1:** the affirmative explanation/proposal.
- **H2:** the strongest specific substantive rival, fairly stated.
- **H3 — residual null:** the record does not warrant selecting either, including insufficient evidence, unresolved conflict, poor transfer, both/neither fitting, or defective boundaries.

Labels are `A1-H1/H2/H3` and optionally `A2-H1/H2/H3`. Percentages concern these assessment outcomes. The unresolved outcome can coexist with either underlying explanation being true. **When the user supplies H3 wording, allocate H3 only under those stated conditions**—do not also dump leftover mass into a broader residual-null. The inferred residual-null definition above applies only when H3 was not supplied. Uncertainty under a narrow supplied H3 does not prove the stronger substantive claim, and does not authorize silently expanding H3.

Record question/scope, rivals, shared predictions, and a discriminating finding. Expose overlap or omitted alternatives; do not silently rewrite the frame. Separate what the user hopes from what the evidence supports only when that distinction matters.

Enable Axis 2 only for a second material question that cannot fit Axis 1's rivals, conditions, or gaps. Complexity, another mechanism, or an unmet condition alone is insufficient. State its contribution and dependence on Axis 1. Separate contrasts are not demonstrated statistical independence: never multiply axis allocations. Respect an explicit one-axis instruction; expose residual complexity inside that axis.

Keep frame versions stable unless meanings/scope materially change. Log activation, deactivation, or reframing; compare only unchanged axes. If more than two questions are needed, name the deferred scope.

Preserve conditional questions: uncertainty about whether an effect exists does not automatically increase uncertainty about its timing *if it exists*. Name any evidence that changes both judgments.

**Investigation warrant:** distinguish supported premises sufficient for an informative test from evidence that the proposed outcome is already established. For each alleged specification blocker, explain why it prevents defining the intervention/exposure, phenotype, measurement, or comparator rather than being something the study can investigate. A prior study’s failure to enroll the proposed subgroup is not itself proof that the subgroup cannot be specified. Do not require completion of the proposed study as a general prerequisite for warranting it. Do not waive an indispensable premise or a genuine feasibility barrier.

**Conjunctive claims:** when a hypothesis requires several conditions, record the decisive conditions separately before allocating. For a design-plus-rationale claim use:

- **S — specification:** required slots `filled / partial / blocked`, with provenance; distinguish proposed from observed values.
- **P — premises:** `supported / partial / speculative / contradicted / unresolved`, naming relevant findings and adverse tests actually encountered; `not tested` remains explicit where applicable.

Assess all required conditions together. Do not invent a numerical cap from qualitative labels or add checklist points. Apply analogous conditions to either rival when relevant; do not force S/P onto unrelated questions.

**When S and P move differently, use this short procedure before allocating:**

1. Name the material changes to S and P, with finding IDs. Distinguish a damaged optional route from a damaged premise required by every survivor.
2. Name the strongest remaining route, or state that none survives. Identify its limiting necessary condition; say when none is unresolved rather than inventing a gap. Complete logistics cannot establish an unsupported premise.
3. Ask whether the S improvement makes a materially supported surviving route more usable, or merely describes how to test an unsupported idea. The latter improves planning but does not itself establish H1.
4. Explain the net direction in one sentence: `S changed because __; P changed because __; the limiting condition is __; H1 rises/falls/stays because __.` For an initial assessment, omit the trend comparison.

Examples are logical checks, not scoring templates:

- Better endpoints plus loss of the only supported mechanism: endpoint detail cannot compensate for that loss. This weakens H1's premise support; an upward allocation needs a separate evidential reason. A small change need not cross a whole-point boundary. Decide H2 versus null from the actual H2 definition.
- Better eligibility criteria plus failure of an optional route: H1 may rise if the criteria remove a real obstacle for another materially supported route. Name that route and its remaining limitations.

If the opposing changes cannot be resolved confidently, say so and keep the allocation provisional. Do not force a direction or use null points as a fixed balancing account.

### B2. Map discriminators and route failures

Read the frozen record, not previous allocations, as evidence. Review changed findings and their dependents; reconsider each affected axis once. Use a selective table:

`Finding IDs | Axis/condition | Effect | Why diagnostic | Main limitation`

Effects: `H1`, `H2`, `SHARED`, `UNRESOLVED`, `FRAME PROBLEM`. Ask which rival makes the finding less surprising and why. Compatibility, study counts, source prestige, repeated prose, and checklist completion are not independent support. Include principal drivers, strongest adverse evidence, and missing necessary bridges; cite background compactly.

Where an explanation offers alternative routes, list only material routes and their decisive links, using `supported / partial / speculative / contradicted / unresolved`. A merely conceivable route is not a supported survivor. Multiple routes sharing one necessary premise do not protect against that premise's failure. Do not expand a route list to rescue a preferred hypothesis.

**Broken links have conditional consequences, not fixed point destinations.** State what failed, its evidence, whether failure is affirmative or merely missing support, the affected route/shared premise, surviving routes, and what the defined H2 predicts.

- Defeating one optional route weakens that route; it does not by itself establish failure of the whole proposal.
- If H2 asserts that no adequate route exists, support for H2 requires evidence addressing that assertion: for example a failed shared necessary link, defeat of the last materially supported route, or a contradiction of a premise every survivor needs. These are scoped evidential reasons, not automatic proof of universal impossibility.
- If H2 is a positive competing explanation, direct evidence favoring it can matter even while an H1 route survives. Do not impose the preceding rejection-only rule on every task.
- Missing support or unresolved conflict can justify more null weight; decisive counterevidence can favor a rival. Neither forces a fixed transfer. Reallocate holistically and explain substantive movements using the actual frame.

Apply transport checks equally to supportive and adverse findings. A negative in one population, endpoint, tissue, exposure, or timescale is not automatically a negative in another. Universal claims such as “no route” or “no benefit” require coverage consistent with their breadth. Mentioning a caveat does not excuse ignoring it in the allocation.

**Before promoting any principal driver, ask the same three questions regardless of its direction:** What was actually observed? Does it apply to this target and endpoint? What assumption bridges any mismatch? Write only the consequential limit in the diagnostic map. For example, slowing further damage and reversing existing damage are different outcomes; failure at one does not erase evidence for the other.

**Routing reminder:** “H2 did not gain” never proves that a particular number of points must move to null. “No trigger fired” is not a universal rule. Explain support for the complete current vector using the frame and surviving evidence.
