RELATIONS (assign exactly one per witness-unit pair; the relation is between what the WITNESS says, accepted as written, and what the UNIT says):
- EQUIVALENT - materially the same claim at the same strength and scope.
- WITNESS_ENTAILS_UNIT - if the witness is accepted as written, the unit follows from it; the witness is at least as strong and specific as the unit. A reported instance establishes a capability or possibility unit ("X can do Y", "at least one route ...") when the instance meets every scope condition the unit states (population, model, exposure level, tissue, timing).
- UNIT_ENTAILS_WITNESS - the unit would imply the witness, but the witness is weaker or less specific. Relevant partial support, not complete coverage.
- PARTIALLY_COVERS - the witness supplies a material part of the unit but neither text implies the other completely. Typical case: the right effect shown in a different population, species, model, exposure or timing than the unit requires (a transfer assumption would be needed), or one link of a multi-link unit.
- CONTRADICTS - the witness and the unit cannot both be accepted as written under the same recorded scope and conditions (opposite polarity on the same predicate at the same scope; conflicting values for the same quantity; an affirmative finding that the unit's claim fails where the unit applies). A null result in a different population/model/exposure is NOT a contradiction; at most it is PARTIALLY_COVERS or NO_SEMANTIC_RELATION.
- NO_SEMANTIC_RELATION - no material support, contradiction, or partial-coverage relationship.
- ABSTAIN - the texts do not permit a reliable decision.

Protected properties to check: attribution (who says it), actor and object, polarity (affirmed/denied), modality (may/must, suggests/shows), conditions, alternatives, identity and role, time, quantity, scope (population, species, model, organ/tissue, dose, route). High word overlap with changed roles is a classic trap. A source REPORTING a claim is weaker than the claim itself. A claim inside a condition is weaker than the unconditional claim. Never infer coverage from metadata, source prestige, or the number of witnesses; judge the text only.

Every judgment needs a TRACE: one or two sentences naming the exact words and the protected properties that justify the relation.
