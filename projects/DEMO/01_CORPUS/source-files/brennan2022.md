# SYNTHETIC — Silicon in cereals: a narrative review of yield evidence (brennan2022)

> Fabricated demonstration source. Not a real study. Do not cite as evidence.

## Abstract

This narrative review summarises reported effects of silicon application on yield
and drought tolerance in cereal crops, with attention to the gap between
controlled and field studies.

## Scope

We reviewed reports published between 2005 and 2021 on wheat, rice and maize. We
did not perform a meta-analysis and did not extract a pooled effect size.

## Findings

Most controlled studies of silicon and drought report improved water relations,
including higher relative water content and lower transpiration. Grain yield
responses in pots are frequently positive but vary with rate and timing. Among
the field studies we located, results are inconsistent: some report no yield
difference, while others report small increases that were not significant. The
largest body of positive yield evidence comes from rice, a crop that accumulates
silicon in much greater quantities than wheat.

## Interpretation

The review concludes that the field evidence in wheat is too inconsistent to
support a general yield claim. The authors recommend field trials that measure
both leaf water relations and grain yield at a stated silicate rate, because most
studies report one or the other.

## Limitations

This was a narrative rather than systematic review. Publication bias is likely,
and many studies did not report the silicon concentration of the applied product.

## Conclusion

There is plausible mechanistic support for a drought-tolerance effect in wheat,
but the field yield evidence is mixed and does not establish a benefit.
