# SYNTHETIC — Foliar silicate and wheat water relations in a controlled pot trial (alvarez2021)

> Fabricated demonstration source. Not a real study. Do not cite as evidence.

## Abstract

We tested whether foliar potassium silicate improves water relations and yield
components in drought-stressed wheat grown in a controlled greenhouse. Plants were
grown in pots and subjected to a imposed terminal drought from anthesis.

## Methods

Winter wheat was grown in 12-litre pots in a randomized complete block design with
48 pots per treatment. Foliar potassium silicate was applied at 2 mL per litre
three times, at tillering, stem elongation and anthesis. Control plants received
an equal volume of water. Drought was imposed by withholding irrigation from
anthesis.

## Results

Foliar silicate increased relative water content of flag leaves by 9.4 percentage
points compared with the water-sprayed control at 14 days after anthesis. Stomatal
conductance was 0.21 mol per square metre per second in treated plants and 0.16 in
controls. Grain yield per pot was 18.7 grams in treated plants and 14.2 grams in
controls, a difference of 32 percent. Thousand-grain weight did not differ
significantly between treatments.

## Discussion

The water-relations benefit is consistent with a role for silica in leaf
epidermal structure. Because the trial used pots and a single cultivar, the yield
difference should not be extrapolated to field conditions without a field test.
We did not measure soil water content to field capacity, and the drought was
imposed abruptly at anthesis.

## Limitations

The experiment was conducted in one greenhouse and one season. Pots restrict root
volume, and the abrupt drought differs from a natural field drought. No field
validation was attempted.
