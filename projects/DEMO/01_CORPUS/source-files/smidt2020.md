# SYNTHETIC — Foliar silicate, lodging and yield in irrigated wheat (smidt2020)

> Fabricated demonstration source. Not a real study. Do not cite as evidence.

## Abstract

A two-season field experiment tested foliar silicate for lodging resistance and
grain yield in irrigated wheat.

## Methods

Winter wheat was sown in 18 square metre plots with four replicates. Foliar
potassium silicate was applied twice at 3 mL per litre. The trial was irrigated to
avoid drought, so water limitation was not a factor. Lodging was scored on a
0 to 9 scale, and grain yield was measured at maturity.

## Results

Silicate treatment reduced lodging score from 4.1 to 2.3 in the first season.
Grain yield was 8.12 tonnes per hectare in treated plots and 7.94 tonnes per
hectare in controls; the difference was not significant. In the second season,
lodging was minimal in both treatments and yield did not differ. Stem wall
thickness was greater in treated plants.

## Discussion

The experiment shows a structural effect of foliar silicate on stem strength
under irrigated conditions. Because the trial was irrigated and yield was not
water-limited, it provides no evidence about drought-stressed yield.

## Limitations

The trial was irrigated and used a single cultivar. It did not measure leaf water
relations under drought, and lodging was uneven across the site.
