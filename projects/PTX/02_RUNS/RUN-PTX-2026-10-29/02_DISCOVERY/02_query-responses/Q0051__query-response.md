## Exact answer

```text
Diagnostic Uncertainty & Management Failures
[CLINICAL] Unmet need in underactive bladder (UAB) is defined by diagnostic uncertainty—lack of a standardized clinical definition, absent quantitative urodynamic thresholds, and heavy symptomatic overlap with BOO and OAB—alongside management failures: conventional parasympathomimetics, α-blockers, and intravesical PGE2 show no clear benefit, leaving patients dependent on supportive catheterization (lee2018.pdf.md, Introduction, New management strategies for underactive bladder).
Functional Findings Supporting Alternatives
Conservative: Timed/double voiding or catheterization reduces post-void residual (PVR) volume and prevents upper urinary tract damage (lee2018.pdf.md, New management strategies for underactive bladder).
Pharmacological: Bethylchol/parasympathomimetics, PGE2, and α-blockers lack evidence of clinical efficacy (lee2018.pdf.md, New management strategies for underactive bladder).
Outlet-Reducing: HoLEP rendered 89% of DU and 63% of acontractile patients catheter-free long-term by reducing outflow resistance, enabling voiding via abdominal straining rather than restoring detrusor power (lee2018.pdf.md, New management strategies for underactive bladder).
Neuromodulation: Sacral neuromodulation (SNM) eliminated catheterization in 69% of patients vs 9% in controls at 6 months, significantly increasing voided volume and reducing PVR (lee2018.pdf.md, New management strategies for underactive bladder).
Trial Contrast for Detrusor Recovery
[INFERRED] Distinguishing genuine detrusor muscle recovery from lowered outlet resistance or straining adaptation requires a trial contrasting active therapy against isolated outflow reduction (or sham) using invasive pressure-flow urodynamics—specifically tracking changes in Bladder Contractility Index (BCI=PdetQmax+5Qmax) versus abdominal pressure (Pabd) and PVR (lee2018.pdf.md, Introduction, New management strategies for underactive bladder). Review-to-primary dependence: lee2018.pdf.md is a narrative review summarizing primary cohort studies (e.g., Lomas 2016, Jonas 2001) (lee2018.pdf.md, REVIEW).
```
