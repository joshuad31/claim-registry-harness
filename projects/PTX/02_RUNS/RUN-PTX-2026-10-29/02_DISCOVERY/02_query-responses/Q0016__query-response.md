Groups & Timing: Controls (n=8), Sepsis alone (n=8), Pretreatment PTX (15 min prior, n=6), Posttreatment PTX (1 h post-onset, n=6), Delayed PTX in established shock (2 h post-onset, n=4), and Control PTX alone (n=3) (ridings1994.pdf.md, EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN).
Cardiopulmonary & Inflammatory Outcomes: [PRECLINICAL] Both pre- and 1-h posttreatment attenuated arterial acidosis, preserved PaO2, improved cardiac index, and prevented BAL protein accumulation and alveolar neutrophil entry (ridings1994.pdf.md, BLOOD GAS MEASUREMENTS, BAL ANALYSIS). Principal Null Findings: [PRECLINICAL] In vivo PTX failed to reduce plasma TNF bioactivity, peripheral neutropenia, CD18 receptor upregulation, lung myeloperoxidase sequestration, or neutrophil superoxide priming (ridings1994.pdf.md, NEUTROPHIL ADHESION RECEPTOR EXPRESSION AND KINETICS, PLASMA TNF ASSAY). In vitro, PTX reduced TNF-induced neutrophil pseudopodia formation on endothelial cells (ridings1994.pdf.md, IN VITRO ELECTRON MICROSCOPY STUDY).
Prevention vs. Treatment Limits: [PRECLINICAL] Pretreatment and early posttreatment (1 h) confer pulmonary protection within a strict "therapeutic window." Delaying PTX to 2 hours into established septic shock triggered uncontrolled, fatal systemic arterial hypotension resulting in 50% mortality (2 of 4 swine died within 1 h) (ridings1994.pdf.md, RESULTS, COMMENT).
Implications for Bladder Dysfunction: [INFERRED] Systemic vascular collapse caused by delayed IV PTX in acute bacterial shock cannot be inferred to govern established bladder dysfunction or detrusor fibrosis.
