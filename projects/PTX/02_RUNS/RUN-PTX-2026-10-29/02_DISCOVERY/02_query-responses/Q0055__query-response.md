Results of the MAPS Randomized Trials
[CLINICAL] Glazener et al. (2011) evaluated four formal 1-to-1 pelvic-floor muscle training (PFMT) sessions versus standard care across two randomized trials in men incontinent 6 weeks post-surgery (glazener2011.pdf.md, ## Summary):
Trial 1 (Radical Prostatectomy, N = 411):
Denominators & Follow-up: Intervention N = 205, Control N = 206; 12-month follow-up was 95% (196 intervention, 195 control) (glazener2011.pdf.md, ## Results, Table 3).
Exercise Uptake: 92% attended visit 1 and 85% completed visit 4 (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 2); 85% intervention vs. 51% control reported performing exercises at 12 months (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 3).
12-Month Leakage: Any incontinence was 76% (148/196) intervention vs. 77% (151/195) control (RR 0.97, 95% CI [0.87, 1.09], p = 0.64); mean ICIQ score was 4.9 vs. 5.4 (p = 0.36) (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 3).
Trial 2 (TURP, N = 442):
Denominators & Follow-up: Intervention N = 220, Control N = 222; 12-month follow-up was 90% (194 intervention, 203 control) (glazener2011.pdf.md, ## Results, Table 3).
Exercise Uptake: 86% attended visit 1 and 72% completed visit 4 (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 2); 63% intervention vs. 30% control reported performing exercises at 12 months (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 3).
12-Month Leakage: Any incontinence was 65% (126/194) intervention vs. 62% (125/203) control (RR 1.06, 95% CI [0.91, 1.23], p = 0.47); mean ICIQ score was 3.9 vs. 4.0 (p = 0.93) (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 3).
Effect of Greater Uptake & Non-Transferability
[CLINICAL] Primary Outcome vs. Uptake: Greater exercise uptake did not improve outcomes; compliance-adjusted treatment-received analyses showed no significant benefit (Trial 1 RR 0.98, p = 0.68; Trial 2 RR 1.05, p = 0.54) (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 4).
