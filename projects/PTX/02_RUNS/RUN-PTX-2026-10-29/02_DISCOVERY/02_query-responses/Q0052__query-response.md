## Exact answer

```text
[CLINICAL] In spinas1991.pdf.md, a randomized trial in 20 healthy male volunteers evaluated E. coli endotoxin alone (n=7), endotoxin + ibuprofen (n=6; 800 mg PO at -120 min and 0 min), or saline control (n=7) (spinas1991.pdf.md, Subjects and study procedure).
Clinical vs. Biomarker Contrasts: Endotoxin alone induced fever, constitutional symptoms (headache, myalgia, nausea, chills), peak TNF-α (369 ± 44 pg/mL at 90 min), peak IL-6 (27 ± 12.8 ng/mL at 120–180 min), and elevated PMNL-elastase (spinas1991.pdf.md, Results). Ibuprofen pretreatment completely abolished clinical symptoms (all 6 pretreated subjects remained asymptomatic) and blunted fever (P<0.001), yet significantly augmented peak TNF-α (627 ± 136 pg/mL at 120 min; P=0.003), peak IL-6 (113 ± 66 ng/mL at 180 min; P=0.05), and PMNL-elastase release (P<0.001) (spinas1991.pdf.md, Effects of ibuprofen pretreatment on plasma cytokine concentrations).
Challenging Marker-as-Function Reasoning: Complete clinical symptom resolution occurring alongside a 1.7-fold increase in TNF-α, a 4-fold increase in IL-6, and heightened leukocyte elastase release proves that circulating cytokine reduction is not required for clinical improvement (spinas1991.pdf.md, Abstract).
Non-Transferrable Limits: [INFERRED] Acute, short-term systemic IV endotoxemia in healthy males cannot be extrapolated to pentoxifylline (PTX) or chronic bladder disease (spinas1991.pdf.md, Discussion). Local detrusor ischemia, bladder outlet obstruction, and voiding parameters (Qmax, PVR) involve organ-specific structural remodeling rather than systemic cyclooxygenase blockade or acute intravascular lipopolysaccharide shock (spinas1991.pdf.md, Discussion).
```
