[PRECLINICAL] In a randomized, blinded porcine trial (n=5 PTX vs. n=5 colloid control), Parker et al. evaluated high-dose IV pentoxifylline (20 mg/kg bolus + 20 mg/kg 1-h infusion) administered simultaneously with colloid resuscitation 1 hour post-insult in female pigs (45–60 kg) subjected to 40% fixed-volume hemorrhage and E. coli peritonitis (parker2000.pdf.md, 2.3. Experimental procedure).
What PTX Changed:
[PRECLINICAL] Lowered systemic vascular resistance index (SVRI fell 58% vs. 39% in controls, P<0.05) and pulmonary vascular resistance index (PVRI fell 50% vs. 42%, P<0.05), blunting arterial pressure recovery (MAP 62 vs. 92 mmHg at 2 h, P<0.01) (parker2000.pdf.md, 3.1. Cardiovascular parameters).
[PRECLINICAL] Worsened septic lactic acidosis at 2 h (lactate 9.6±1.4 vs. 4.6±1.5 mmol/L, P<0.01; arterial base excess −1.5±0.5 vs. +2.9±1.1 mmol/L, P<0.05) (parker2000.pdf.md, 3.3. Acid–base status).
What PTX Failed to Change:
[PRECLINICAL] Failed to improve 24-h survival (median survival 16 h vs. 11 h; 24-h survivors: 2/5 vs. 1/5, P=NS) (parker2000.pdf.md, 3. Results).
[PRECLINICAL] Failed to suppress peak serum TNF-α levels (parker2000.pdf.md, 3.4. Markers of inflammation and TNF-α Results).
[PRECLINICAL] Failed to preserve renal function, producing significantly worse urine output at 3 h (0.63±0.74 vs. 1.28±0.18 mL/kg/h, P<0.01) (parker2000.pdf.md, 3.1. Cardiovascular parameters).
Narrow Lesson: [INFERRED] Reduced vascular resistance or PDE inhibition does not establish improved organ perfusion; uncompensated acute vasodilation can exacerbate tissue ischemia and organ dysfunction.
