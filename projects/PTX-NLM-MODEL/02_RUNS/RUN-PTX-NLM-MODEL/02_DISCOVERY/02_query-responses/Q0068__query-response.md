6. Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence Type Differentiation:
[PRECLINICAL]: In 6 healthy adult dogs, 29-day oral PTX (20 mg/kg q8h) selectively suppresses IgE-mediated macroscopic late-phase wheal induration at 6 hours (P < 0.05) and 24 hours (P < 0.05), total dermal inflammatory cell recruitment (-55.4%, P < 0.01), and eosinophil influx (-84.2%, P < 0.01), while failing to inhibit 20-minute immediate wheal formation (P > 0.05) or neutrophil influx (P > 0.05).
[MECHANISM]: Nonselective phosphodiesterase inhibition raises intracellular cAMP in mast cells and immunocytes, blunting complete mast cell degranulation and suppressing downstream chemotactic mediator release (IL-13, CCL2, CCL5, PGD2) and adhesion molecule expression, thereby halting late-phase eosinophil recruitment.
[INFERRED]: Oral PTX represents a valuable nonsteroidal adjunctive treatment candidate for canine IgE-mediated allergic skin diseases (such as atopic dermatitis), but requires continuous daily dosing due to rapid loss of efficacy within 7 days of drug withdrawal.
[NO SOURCE]: Plasma parent PTX or active metabolite concentrations (M1, M4, M5) and cutaneous tissue cytokine/adhesion molecule mRNA levels were not measured in this study.
Key Implications for Veterinary & Translational Practice:
PTX selectively targets late-phase allergic inflammation (eosinophil influx and tissue induration) without impairing immediate histamine/vasoactive amine release.
Discontinuing PTX leads to full recovery of allergic cutaneous reactivity within 1 week, reinforcing the necessity of strict compliance during clinical management.
