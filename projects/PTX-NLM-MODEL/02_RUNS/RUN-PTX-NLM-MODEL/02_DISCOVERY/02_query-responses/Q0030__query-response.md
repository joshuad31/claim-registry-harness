3. Synthesis and Key Implications
Comparative Evidence Synthesis
[CLINICAL] The two trials reveal a clear divergence in cerebral perfusion outcomes based on baseline vascular pathology and route of administration:
Ischemic Cerebrovascular Disease: In patients with focal ischemic stroke or RIND, single oral doses of sustained-release PTX (400 mg or 800 mg) significantly increased regional perfusion in the most ischemic brain regions (+43% to +44% lowest rCBF increase, p≤0.038), while 800 mg significantly elevated global CBF (+16% to +25%, p=0.017)

Mechanistic Bridges and Transfer Assumptions
[MECHANISM] In healthy cerebral vessels, PTX acts primarily as a cAMP-selective phosphodiesterase inhibitor, increasing vascular smooth muscle cAMP to produce mild large-artery vasodilation without altering parenchymal metabolic flow demand
21
27
 (kruuse2000.pdf.md, Main study, Discussion). In contrast, in diseased cerebrovascular beds, the microcirculatory recruitment by PTX is driven by hemorheological actions (reducing blood viscosity, improving erythrocyte deformability, and inhibiting neutrophil adhesion) that lower microvascular resistance in maximally dilated ischemic territories

28
 (bowton1989.pdf.md, Introduction, Discussion).
