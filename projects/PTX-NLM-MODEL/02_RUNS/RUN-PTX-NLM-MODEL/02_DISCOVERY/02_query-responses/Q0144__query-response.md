[MECHANISM] Unchanged Transduction Pathways: 5–50 \mu M parent PTX does not alter resting or FMLP-stimulated intracellular calcium transients (resting 88 \pm 10 nmol/L, stimulated 799 \pm 197 nmol/L), does not increase resting or stimulated intracellular cAMP, and does not block GM-CSF priming of the respiratory burst (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results & Discussion).
Intermediate Micromolar Range (50–500 \mu M / ~14–139 \mu g/mL):
[PRECLINICAL] Parent PTX at 100 \mu M shortens FMLP respiratory burst duration from 177 \pm 26 s to 124 \pm 14 s (p=0.015); 500 \mu M shortens burst duration to 114 \pm 12 s (p=0.003), reducing total superoxide to 35% of control (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results).

Exposure Regime Mapping:
The oral regimen of 400 mg q8h occupies the low micromolar exposure regime (~1 to 5 \mu M peak parent PTX plasma concentration), accompanied by circulating Metabolite-1 levels in the 3 to 10 \mu M range (staubach1998.pdf.md, RESULTS; roberts1993.pdf.md, Abstract). It does not achieve intermediate (50–500 \mu M) or millimolar (>1 mM) systemic exposures (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results).
Mechanisms That CAN Be Validly Claimed:
Inhibition of Peptide-Stimulated Oxidative Burst: Parent PTX concentrations of 5–10 \mu M directly attenuate FMLP-stimulated PMN H₂O₂ and superoxide production (75–76% of control) (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results & Fig. 1a, 1b).
