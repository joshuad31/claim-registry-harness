1. Delanian et al., 2003 (2x2 Factorial Design)
What it CAN Separate:
Single-Agent Efficacy vs. Double Placebo: Uniquely isolates the marginal effect of pentoxifylline alone (Group B: -39.1%) and vitamin E alone (Group C: -40.0%) against double placebo (Group D: -42.6%) (delanian2003.pdf.md, Table 3).
True Pharmacological Synergy: Proves that single-agent therapy is ineffective (neither PTX nor Vit E alone beat double placebo), whereas combined administration achieves a statistically significant 60.2% regression (p=0.038), establishing true synergistic co-action between PDE inhibition and ROS scavenging (delanian2003.pdf.md, RESULTS / Primary Analyses & DISCUSSION).
What it CANNOT Separate:
Long-Term Regression & Rebound: Cannot evaluate whether 6 months is sufficient to prevent fibrotic relapse after drug withdrawal, nor can it map regression kinetics beyond 6 months (delanian2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
2. Delanian et al., 2005 (Longitudinal Duration Cohort)
What it CAN Separate:
Treatment Duration & Maximal Response Time: Isolates the effect of short (6–12 mo) vs. long (24–48 mo) treatment duration, proving that anti-fibrotic tissue remodeling requires a mean of 24 months (2 years) to achieve two-thirds maximal response (delanian2005.pdf.md, ABSTRACT & Table 2).
Off-Drug Rebound Relapse Rates: Quantifies post-discontinuation rebound, demonstrating that premature drug cessation (<12 mo) leads to a severe 40%/year rebound rate, whereas long-term treatment (≥24–36 mo) stabilizes tissue and reduces rebound to 8.5%/year (p=0.0012) (delanian2005.pdf.md, RESULTS / Primary Analyses).
What it CANNOT Separate:
Single-Agent Monotherapy Contributions: Cannot determine the relative contribution of PTX vs. Vit E, as all patients received combined therapy without a placebo control arm (delanian2005.pdf.md, PATIENTS AND METHODS / Modalities of Combined PTX-vitE Treatment).
