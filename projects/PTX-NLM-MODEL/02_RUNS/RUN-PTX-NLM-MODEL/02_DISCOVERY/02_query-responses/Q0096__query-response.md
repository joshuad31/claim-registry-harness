A. Chung (2014) (chung2014.pdf.md)
Definition: [CLINICAL] Defines paruresis (or "bashful bladder") as a psychogenic inhibition of micturition characterized by difficulty or inability to urinate in situations where others are present, or may soon be present (chung2014.pdf.md, Exact Definition and Proposed Pathophysiologic Mechanisms).
Proposed Mechanism: [MECHANISM] Categorizes paruresis as a psychogenic etiology within the broad spectrum of non-obstructive voiding dysfunction (chung2014.pdf.md, Exact Definition and Proposed Pathophysiologic Mechanisms).
B. Osman et al. (2018) (osman2018.pdf.md)
Definition: [CLINICAL] Terms situational voiding failure during formal testing as "bashful bladder"—a condition where a patient is unable to void during a urodynamic study due to situational anxiety (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics). Open peer-review commentary by Rosier highlights upcoming International Continence Society (ICS) terminology describing this phenomenon as "situational inability to void" or "situational inability to void as usual" (osman2018.pdf.md, Open Peer Review).
Proposed Mechanism: [MECHANISM] Attributes bashful bladder to situational anxiety that induces inadequate relaxation of the striated external urethral sphincter (rhabdosphincter) combined with secondary reflex detrusor inhibition (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics).
C. Kaplan et al. (1997) (kaplan1997.pdf.md)
Definition: [CLINICAL] Frames situational voiding inhibition under the clinical entity "pseudodyssynergia" (or "bashful voiding" / performance anxiety), defined as voluntary or stress-induced contraction of the external urinary sphincter during voiding in neurologically intact individuals (kaplan1997.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; MATERIALS AND METHODS). It is explicitly differentiated from neurogenic detrusor-external sphincter dyssynergia (DSD) seen in spinal cord injury (kaplan1997.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Proposed Mechanism: [MECHANISM] Occurs secondary to voluntary increases in sphincteric activity during attempted suppression of an uninhibited contraction, abdominal straining, or localized pelvic discomfort (kaplan1997.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). The underlying drive is attributed to anxiety, emotional stress, or mislearned voiding behaviors acquired during childhood toilet training (kaplan1997.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
3. Recommended Diagnostic Methods
A. Chung (2014) (chung2014.pdf.md)
Invasive Urodynamics: [CLINICAL] Recommends invasive pressure-flow urodynamic studies (PFS) as the sole definitive method to assess detrusor contractile function and differentiate UAB from bladder outlet obstruction (BOO) (chung2014.pdf.md, Making the Diagnosis).
Non-Invasive Diagnostic Tools: Mentions non-invasive contractility assessments—such as inflatable penile cuff interruption, external condom catheter filling, and current perception threshold electrical sensation testing—as experimental or emerging techniques (chung2014.pdf.md, Making the Diagnosis).
B. Osman et al. (2018) (osman2018.pdf.md)
Clinical Screening: [CLINICAL] Recommends taking a thorough clinical history of previous voiding patterns to identify pre-existing situational anxiety (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics).
Ambulatory Urodynamics: [CLINICAL] Recommends ambulatory urodynamic monitoring when situational inhibition prevents voiding during standard laboratory cystometry, allowing evaluation in a natural environment (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics).
C. Kaplan et al. (1997) (kaplan1997.pdf.md)
Triple-Criteria Video Urodynamic Protocol: [CLINICAL] Establishes a rigorous diagnostic protocol to confirm pseudodyssynergia and rule out testing artifacts (kaplan1997.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; MATERIALS AND METHODS):
Synchronous Video Pressure-Flow EMG: Surface or needle electromyography demonstrating increased electrical activity of the external sphincter during voiding without abdominal straining (kaplan1997.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Fluoroscopic Imaging: Radiographic visualization demonstrating brief, intermittent closing or contrast cutoff at the level of the membranous urethral external sphincter (kaplan1997.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Uncatheterized Private Uroflowmetry: Uroflowmetry performed in a private room showing intermittent, undulating flow curves, specifically required to exclude performance anxiety / "bashful voiding" artifacts generated by the examiner's presence (kaplan1997.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).

A. Claims Grounded in Cited Studies
Psychogenic Formulation: Chung (2014) grounds the clinical definition of paruresis directly in Boschen (2008) (chung2014.pdf.md, Exact Definition and Proposed Pathophysiologic Mechanisms).
Ambulatory Urodynamic Utility: Osman et al. (2018) ground the recommendation for ambulatory urodynamics in bashful bladder patients on published studies by Rosario et al. (2000) and van Koeveringe et al. (2010) (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics).
Biofeedback Efficacy in Pseudodyssynergia: Kaplan et al. (1997) provide primary empirical data showing that 83% of men with external sphincter pseudodyssynergia respond to biofeedback pelvic floor relaxation (AUA symptom scores decreased from 18.4 to 7.6 at 3 months, P<0.001) (kaplan1997.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; RESULTS). Their conceptual framework rests on established pediatric/adult literature on non-neurogenic voiding dysfunction (Hinman & Bauman 1973; Maizels et al. 1979, 1983; Hellström et al. 1987) (kaplan1997.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
B. Claims Resting on Author Opinion or Uncited Speculation
Long-Term Progression to Underactive Bladder: [INFERRED] Chung (2014) states that paruresis "may contribute to UAB in the long term" (chung2014.pdf.md, Exact Definition and Proposed Pathophysiologic Mechanisms). This is purely author opinion. No longitudinal cohort studies or primary data are cited to support the claim that psychogenic situational inhibition causes progressive myogenic or neurogenic detrusor failure (chung2014.pdf.md, Exact Definition and Proposed Pathophysiologic Mechanisms).
Reflex Mechanism Narrative & Ambulatory Reliability: [INFERRED] Osman et al. (2018) describe the "rhabdosphincter relaxation failure + reflex detrusor inhibition" mechanism conversationally without citing direct neurophysiological recording trials in that paragraph (osman2018.pdf.md, Ambulatory urodynamics). Peer reviewer Rosier explicitly notes that attributing diagnostic reliability to ambulatory urodynamics in bashful bladder "remains speculative... because of the lack of evidence" (osman2018.pdf.md, Open Peer Review).
Firstborn Birth-Order Psychodynamics: [INFERRED] Kaplan et al. (1997) note a >90% prevalence of firstborn men in their pseudodyssynergia cohort and hypothesize that this reflects overzealous toilet training, parental expectations, and perfectionist stress (kaplan1997.pdf.md, DISCUSSION). This is explicit author speculation; the authors acknowledge that these psychological phenomena have not been formally tested or validated in adult urological populations (kaplan1997.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
6. Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence Type Differentiation:
[CLINICAL]: Pseudodyssynergia / situational voiding inhibition is an established cause of non-obstructive voiding dysfunction in young men (Kaplan 1997, N=43), which is misdiagnosed as chronic prostatitis and successfully treated with pelvic floor biofeedback (83% response rate).
[MECHANISM]: Performance anxiety triggers voluntary or unlearned striated external sphincter contraction during micturition, resulting in undulating uroflow curves, high voiding pressures, and secondary reflex detrusor inhibition.
[INFERRED]: Labeling paruresis as an etiology of chronic "underactive bladder" reflects conceptual classification rather than proven structural detrusor failure.
[NO SOURCE]: None of the three sources present prospective longitudinal data demonstrating that paruresis leads to irreversible detrusor collagen fibrosis or permanent loss of detrusor contractility.
Key Implications for Practice:
Young men presenting with chronic voiding difficulty and pelvic pain should undergo private uroflowmetry and video pressure-flow EMG to rule out pseudodyssynergia before receiving empirical antibiotics or surgery.
Behavioral retraining and biofeedback provide high success rates in situational voiding dysfunction without requiring invasive interventions.
