5. Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence Type Differentiation:
[CLINICAL]: Reviews confirm that parasympathomimetics lack clinical efficacy and carry systemic risks; sacral neuromodulation (SNM) and urethral BTX-A achieve high response rates in non-obstructive/neurogenic retention (69–97%), but prostatectomy (TURP) in established detrusor underactivity fails to improve 10-year voiding efficiency (58% vs 82%, P=0.049).
[MECHANISM]: BPO-induced detrusor underactivity progresses from initial compensatory hypertrophy to chronic ischemic remodeling (HIF-1α, TGF-β1), characterized by myocyte apoptosis, muscarinic receptor loss, and dense intercellular collagen fibrosis.
[INFERRED]: Interventions targeting neurogenic signal amplification (parasympathomimetics, SNM) or functional sphincter relaxation (BTX-A) fail in non-neurogenic older men because the primary limiting pathology is irreversible target-organ myogenic destruction rather than neural signaling failure.
[NO SOURCE]: Neither review evaluated pentoxifylline (PTX) combined with α
1
	​

-blockers in human BPO/DU cohorts, nor did they establish non-invasive biomarker cutoffs for detrusor fibrosis.
Key Implications for Clinical Practice:
Preoperative urodynamic pressure-flow testing (or non-invasive detrusor wall thickness combined with bladder capacity) is essential prior to prostate surgery in older men to identify detrusor underactivity and avoid futile TURP.
Therapeutic research in geriatric UAB must shift from simple neurogenic/cholinergic stimulation toward early anti-ischemic, anti-inflammatory, and antifibrotic interventions designed to preserve detrusor microvascular perfusion and prevent irreversible structural remodeling.
