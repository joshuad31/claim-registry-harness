Measurement Tool: Assessed adherence using weekly patient self-report logs recording the daily number of pelvic floor exercise sessions and contractions across 4 clinic visits (baseline, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th visits; mean duration between 1st and 4th visit was 213.40 days / 30.50 weeks) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS & RESULTS). Grading Scale: Classified adherence into a 3-point ordinal scale (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS):
Grade 1 = Low compliance (completed <20% of the recommended exercise regimen) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Grade 2 = Moderate compliance (completed 20% to 80% of the recommended exercise regimen) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Grade 3 = High compliance (completed >80% of the recommended exercise regimen) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).

2. Outcome Associations, Values, and Statistics
[CLINICAL] Siu et al. (2003) (siu2003.pdf.md):
Overall Treatment Response: In a cohort of 214 Chinese women with urinary stress incontinence, the pelvic muscle training program reduced overall wetting episodes by 85.2% and increased pelvic muscle strength by 73.1% (siu2003.pdf.md, Abstract).
Path Analysis & Stepwise Regression:
Incontinence episode severity displayed strong autoregression across visits: baseline to 2nd visit (path coefficient = 0.81), 2nd to 3rd visit (0.77), and 3rd to 4th visit (0.95) (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS / Fig. 1).
Higher compliance directly reduced incontinence episode frequency: 2nd visit compliance to 2nd visit incontinence (path coefficient = -0.28), 3rd visit compliance to 3rd visit incontinence (-0.25), and 4th visit compliance to 4th visit incontinence (-0.32) (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS / Fig. 1).
In early visits, compliance improved pelvic muscle strength (2nd visit path = -0.31; 3rd visit path = -0.24; strength was inversely correlated with incontinence) (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS / Fig. 1 & DISCUSSION).
In later visits, compliance strongly enhanced correct exercise execution technique (3rd visit compliance to 3rd visit technique path = 0.73; 4th visit path = -0.28) (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS / Fig. 1 & DISCUSSION).
Non-Significant Confounders: Age, parity, mode of delivery, delivery of a large baby (>4 kg), menopausal status, pelvic surgery history, constipation, comorbidity, and baseline incontinence duration did not significantly predict incontinence severity (siu2003.pdf.md, Abstract & MATERIALS AND METHODS).

3. Attrition Rates, Reasons, and Underlying Causal Assumptions
[CLINICAL] Siu et al. (2003) (siu2003.pdf.md):
Attrition Profile: Enrolled 214 women at baseline (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS). The authors report a "high attrition rate" across the 4 clinic visits spanning a mean of 213.4 days, noting that a significant proportion dropped out (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
Causal Assumptions:
Modeled a sequential causal chain assuming exercise compliance directly increases muscle strength and technical correctness, which sequentially causes incontinence reduction (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS & Fig. 1).
Confounding Risk: Because compliance was self-reported and unblinded, the assumption of unidirectional causality is vulnerable to reverse causation (e.g., women experiencing rapid early symptom relief may become more compliant, or unmotivated non-responders may selectively drop out) (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
[CLINICAL] Alewijnse et al. (2003) (alewijnse2003.pdf.md):
Attrition Profile: Enrolled 129 women; 4 declined before starting therapy (3 due to intercurrent medical issues) (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results / Participants and non-response). 26 women (20.2% of 129) were lost to follow-up (10 before therapy, 4 during therapy, 12 during post-therapy follow-up) (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results / Participants and non-response).
Reasons for Drop-out: Personal illness (n=12), ill relative (n=3), stressful/busy schedule (n=8), transport issues (n=2), perceived lack of symptom improvement (n=2), lack of interest (n=7), and death (n=1) (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results / Participants and non-response).
Systematic Attrition Bias: Women lost to follow-up had significantly more frequent (Mann-Whitney U=978.5,P=0.029) and more severe (U=1036.5,P=0.045) baseline wet episodes than completers (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results / Participants and non-response).
Causal Assumptions vs. Theoretical Models:
Failure of Intention Mediation: Contrary to the Attitude–Social influence–self-Efficacy (ASE) model assumption, pre-test intention failed to predict long-term adherence (β=−0.095,P=0.368) due to a ceiling effect (intention was uniformly high, mean score 12.8/14) (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Table II & Discussion).
Habituation Model: Short-term adherence directly predicted long-term adherence (β=0.428,P<0.001), supporting an unmediated habituation process where early routine execution bypasses conscious cognitive intentions (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).
Symptom-Driven Feedback: Baseline leakage frequency positively predicted 12-month adherence (β=0.276,P=0.010), demonstrating that ongoing symptom bother serves as a continuous reinforcement cue for sustained exercise effort (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).
4. Design Changes for Valid Adherence Inference
[INFERRED] To establish valid causal inferences regarding pelvic floor muscle exercise adherence, future trial designs must implement specific structural modifications (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion & Implications for practice):
Objective Adherence Verification: Self-reported exercise logs in both studies introduced potential reporting bias, Hawthorne effects, and social desirability bias (Alewijnse et al. documented high social desirability scores of 80%) (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion). Valid inference requires replacing paper diaries with objective electronic logging systems (e.g., intra-vaginal pressure/s-EMG sensors with automated time-stamped digital memory) (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).
Handling Differential Attrition: Complete-case analysis in the presence of severe attrition (Siu et al.) or systematic dropout of severely symptomatic women (Alewijnse et al.) skews effect estimates (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results & Discussion). Studies must pre-specify intention-to-treat (ITT) analyses using multiple imputation or pattern-mixture models to prevent attrition bias (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).
Isolating Protocol Standardization Effects: Alewijnse et al. observed that therapist adherence to a written protocol checklist produced unexpectedly high long-term patient adherence (67%), neutralizing the added value of the experimental self-help guide (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Implications for practice). Validating this effect requires a randomized controlled trial comparing standardized protocol-guided therapy against unstandardized usual care (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Implications for practice).
Disentangling Reverse Causality: Longitudinal path models must incorporate instrumental variable approaches or cross-lagged structural equation modeling to separate whether adherence drives symptom improvement or whether early symptom response dynamically reinforces exercise adherence (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).

Synthesis and Key Implications
[CLINICAL] Predictors of Long-Term Exercise Adherence: Findings across both cohort studies confirm that long-term adherence to pelvic floor muscle exercises is primarily driven by short-term past adherence habits (β=0.428,P<0.001) and ongoing baseline symptom severity (β=0.276,P=0.010), rather than initial cognitive intentions or demographic variables (siu2003.pdf.md, Abstract; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Table III & Discussion).
[MECHANISM] Dual Physical and Behavioral Feedback Loops:
Neuromuscular Reinforcement: Early exercise compliance increases objective pelvic floor muscle strength and isolated contraction accuracy, directly blunting stress leakage episodes (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
Habituation & Symptom Cues: Long-term exercise persistence operates through an unmediated habituation process (bypassing conscious intention) where higher baseline wet episode frequency acts as a continuous behavioral prompt to sustain daily training (alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Discussion).
[INFERRED] Methodological Imperatives for Clinical Trials: Evaluating exercise adherence requires eliminating self-report social desirability bias through objective digital sensor monitoring, accounting for differential drop-out in severely symptomatic patients via ITT imputation, and utilizing cross-lagged structural equation modeling to isolate true treatment effects from reverse symptom-driven causality (siu2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION; alewijnse2003.pdf.md, Results & Implications for practice).
