Unmasking Hidden Efficacy versus Averaging Out Treatment Effects:
Raison 2013 demonstrates that when a disease process is biologically heterogeneous (e.g., TRD with or without systemic inflammation), analyzing the population as a single unstratified group yields a false-negative result (overall treatment difference = 1.2 HAM-D points, P=.92) (raison2013.pdf.md, RESULTS). Only by pre-specifying baseline biomarker stratification and testing formal interaction terms (P=.01) could the investigators discover that infliximab exerts a large therapeutic benefit in the high-inflammation subgroup (hs-CRP > 5 mg/L, d=0.41, NNT = 3.45) while causing potential harm or placebo-inferiority in non-inflamed patients (hs-CRP ≤ 5 mg/L, d=−0.82, NNH = 6.25) (raison2013.pdf.md, RESULTS; Figure 4; COMMENT).

Protection Against Post-Hoc Data Dredging and Power Loss:
Pre-specifying responder subgroups in the statistical analysis plan (as in Raison 2013) preserves methodological rigor and protects against spurious post-hoc findings (raison2013.pdf.md, COMMENT). However, Raison 2013 also illustrates the statistical power penalty of subgrouping: dividing a 60-patient trial into hs-CRP > 5 mg/L (n=22) and ≤5 mg/L (n=38) reduced subgroup sample sizes, causing responder rate differences (62% vs 33%) to lose statistical significance (P=.19) despite a large standardized effect size (d=0.41) (raison2013.pdf.md, RESULTS; COMMENT).
Together, both sources imply that future trials evaluating immunomodulatory or hemorheological agents (like PTX or anticytokines) must pre-specify baseline responder subgroups in their protocol AND adequately power the study sample size to analyze interaction effects within those target subgroups.

Key Implications for Practice:
Biological targeted therapies (like TNF inhibitors) require pre-treatment biomarker screening (e.g. hs-CRP > 5 mg/L) to select appropriate patients and avoid treating non-inflamed individuals.
Add-on pentoxifylline combined with tamsulosin provides robust clinical and objective flow improvements in symptomatic BPH/LUTS, but larger stratified trials are needed to identify optimal baseline responder profiles.
