2. Conclusions in shah2016.pdf.md
Dissociation of Inflammation from Structural Fat Loss: The authors conclude that there is a total disconnect between structural/metabolic normalization and inflammatory biomarker resolution (shah2016.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; DISCUSSION).
Persistent Low-Grade Inflammatory State: Surgical remission achieved dramatic anatomical and metabolic recovery—significantly reducing BMI (P<0.001), waist circumference (P<0.001), HOMA-IR (P<0.001), visceral fat (P<0.001), trunk subcutaneous fat (P<0.001), inter-muscular fat (P=0.04), and hepatic fat (P=0.03) (shah2016.pdf.md, Table 3; Figure 1 & 3). However, these structural reductions failed to lower circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL−6,P=0.66; IL−1β,P=0.20), which remained as high as in active disease (shah2016.pdf.md, Table 2; Figure 2).
Clinical Significance: The authors conclude that chronic low-grade inflammation persists independently of fat depot regression, likely due to irreversible "metabolic memory" or glucocorticoid receptor transcriptional priming, providing a pathophysiological explanation for why cured Cushing's disease patients maintain elevated long-term cardiovascular mortality risks (shah2016.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; DISCUSSION).

[MECHANISM] The combined findings of roerink2019.pdf.md and shah2016.pdf.md demonstrate that long-term pre-exposure to glucocorticoid excess leaves persistent functional and immunological "scars" that survive biochemical cure (roerink2019.pdf.md, Discussion; shah2016.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
