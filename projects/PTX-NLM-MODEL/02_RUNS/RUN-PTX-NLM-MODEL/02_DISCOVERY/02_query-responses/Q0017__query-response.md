Synthesis and Key Implications
[PRECLINICAL] Selective Inhibitory Profile at Therapeutic Doses: Clinically achievable concentrations of pentoxifylline (5–10 μmol/l) selectively attenuate bacterial chemotactic peptide (FMLP)-induced neutrophil activation—specifically reducing respiratory burst superoxide output (P<0.05 at ≥5 μmol/l) and directed migration under agarose (P=0.027 at 10 μmol/l) (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results).
[MECHANISM] Dissociation from Classical PDE / cAMP / Calcium Pathways: Therapeutic concentrations of PTX (5–10 μmol/l) do not elevate baseline cAMP, do not enhance FMLP-stimulated cAMP transients (which requires ≥100 μmol/l), and do not alter FMLP-induced [Ca
2+
]
i
	​

 flux (which requires 500 μmol/l) (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results / Mechanism of action of pentoxifylline). Thus, the anti-neutrophil effects of PTX at therapeutic levels occur independently of classical phosphodiesterase inhibition or calcium channel block (roberts1993.pdf.md, Discussion).
[PRECLINICAL] Preservation of Cytokine-Mediated Host Defense: PTX at therapeutic concentrations does not suppress GM-CSF- or TNFα-mediated priming of the respiratory burst, does not block GM-CSF-induced cell-surface CD11b expression, and does not impair GM-CSF-driven neutrophil margination in vivo (roberts1993.pdf.md, Results & Discussion).
[INFERRED] Clinical Safety Implications: The retention of cytokine-stimulated phagocyte functions indicates that co-administering pentoxifylline to reduce tissue toxicity (e.g., post-transplant organ injury) will not compromise essential neutrophil host defense responses to infectious pathogens (roberts1993.pdf.md, Abstract & Discussion).
