[INFERRED] Critical methodological and pathophysiological boundaries must be emphasized:
Renal Perfusion Disconnect: As proven in kim2001.pdf.md, PTX's protection of GFR and tubular transport occurs without restoring post-ischemic renal blood flow (RBF remained severely depressed at 7 mL/min vs 6 mL/min in controls), demonstrating that renal protection is mediated by direct parenchymal/anti-inflammatory actions rather than renal macrovascular vasodilation (kim2001.pdf.md, RESULTS > Effect of PTX on Renal Blood Flow; DISCUSSION).
Absence of Direct Antioxidant Action: In vitro slice assays in kim2001.pdf.md proved that PTX does not prevent oxidant-induced lipid peroxidation (MDA formation was uninhibited by PTX vs DPPD), confirming that its renal protection is not driven by direct free-radical scavenging (kim2001.pdf.md, Table 2; DISCUSSION).
Absolute Requirement for Pre-Reperfusion Timing: Both studies administered PTX prior to blood reflow (3 days pre-treatment in hafez2012.pdf.md; 10 min pre-reperfusion in kim2001.pdf.md). Neither study established therapeutic efficacy for post-reperfusion administration ([NO SOURCE]).
