3. Implications for Choosing Functional vs. Biomarker Endpoints
[INFERRED] Synthesizing evidence across these three clinical studies demonstrates that functional performance and organ-level physiologic endpoints are vastly superior to circulating biomarkers or localized tissue histology for defining true therapeutic efficacy in clinical trials:
Circulating Soluble Biomarkers Disconnect from Clinical Efficacy:
In severe sepsis (staubach1998.pdf.md), IV pentoxifylline produced significant, clinically meaningful improvements in organ failure (MOD score), lung oxygenation (PaO
2
	​

/FiO

), and cardiovascular performance (PAHR) without causing any detectable drop in circulating TNF-α, IL-6, or endotoxin levels (staubach1998.pdf.md, RESULTS & Table 2).
In Cushing's disease remission (shah2016.pdf.md), complete surgical cure and anatomical fat loss failed to normalize circulating IL-6 and IL-1β, while hs-CRP levels were non-informative (shah2016.pdf.md, ABSTRACT & RESULTS).
Trial Implication: Selecting circulating cytokines as primary screening endpoints risks discarding effective therapies (Type II error) or misclassifying cured patients as treatment failures.
Localized Tissue Biopsies Can Miss Systemic Functional Impairments:
In cured Cushing's syndrome (roerink2019.pdf.md), skeletal muscle tissue biopsies of the vastus lateralis were completely normal across fiber size, capillarization, mitochondrial content, and eNOS/NOX2 expression (roerink2019.pdf.md, Abstract). However, cardiopulmonary exercise testing revealed a severe 20% deficit in VO
