6. Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence Type Differentiation:
[CLINICAL]: A 12-item Rehabilitation Training Compliance Scale demonstrates high psychometric reliability (Cronbach's α=0.95, test-retest r=0.86) in 123 UI patients. Systematic review meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (N=2,190) shows PFMT significantly improves short-term quality of life (SMD = -0.12, p=0.036), but does not reduce short-term UI severity (SMD = 0.07, p=0.813).
[MECHANISM]: Behavioral pelvic floor muscle training and timed voiding reinforce cortical-brainstem reflex control and urethral sphincter closure, but therapeutic benefits depend on sustained motor habituation.
[INFERRED]: Measuring exercise compliance via unvalidated verbal reports or unstandardized logs creates contradictory trial results (fulfilling all 4 Nagpal scenarios), necessitating standardized, objective compliance tools to establish true dose-response relationships.
[NO SOURCE]: Neither source evaluated digital sensor-based objective tracking or pentoxifylline pharmacotherapy co-administration.
Key Implications for Clinical Practice:
Clinicians should adopt validated multi-dimensional compliance scales (assessing PFMT, bladder training, and diary habits) rather than binary verbal checks.
Longitudinal booster strategies (app reminders, follow-up calls) are essential to prevent the steep decay in exercise adherence observed beyond 3 to 6 months.
