1. Adherence Measurement Methodology
[CLINICAL] In siu2003.pdf.md, adherence (termed "compliance" throughout the text) was quantified through self-reported patient exercise logs carried between clinic visits (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). Patients recorded the number of daily exercise sessions and individual muscle contractions completed on a weekly charting form (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
[CLINICAL] The prescribed pelvic floor muscle exercise (PFME) protocol instructed women to perform 30 contractions per day divided into three equal sessions (10 contractions each in the morning, afternoon, and before retiring) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). Each contraction involved a selective voluntary pelvic muscle squeeze held for 5 seconds, followed by 5 seconds of relaxation, performed in sitting, standing, or lying positions while consciously avoiding abdominal, gluteal, or thigh co-contractions (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
[CLINICAL] Patient self-reports were appraised against the standard 30-contraction/day prescription and categorized into a 3-point ordinal compliance grade (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS):
High Compliance (Grade 3): Executed >80% of the recommended exercise regimen (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Moderate Compliance (Grade 2): Executed between 20% and 80% of the recommended exercise regimen (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
Low Compliance (Grade 1): Executed <20% of the recommended exercise regimen (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).
[CLINICAL] At each monthly return visit, the nurse specialist reviewed patient exercise logs, conducted a physical assessment, and delivered individual verbal feedback, encouragement, and technique reinforcement (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). Overall compliance prevalence among retained participants increased from 84.0% at the first visit to 94.4% at the fourth clinic visit (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS).
2. Primary Clinical Outcomes and Follow-Up Schedule
[CLINICAL] Study Cohort & Baseline Characteristics: The trial recruited 214 Chinese women presenting with stress urinary incontinence (SUI; defined as ≥3 wetting episodes per month) at a hospital continence clinic in Hong Kong between September 1997 and December 2000 (siu2003.pdf.md, Aim; MATERIALS AND METHODS). For the total cohort (N = 214), the mean age was 52.02 ± 11.30 years, mean SUI duration was 5.34 ± 4.66 years, and mean parity was 3.02 ± 1.77 (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS). The vast majority had normal vaginal deliveries (96.2%), 14.2% delivered a infant >4 kg, 33.3% were menopausal, and 83% had no comorbid medical illnesses (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS).
[CLINICAL] Follow-Up Timeline: The rehabilitation program comprised four clinic sessions scheduled at monthly intervals (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). The mean total elapsed time between the baseline first visit and the final fourth visit was 213.40 days (approximately 30.50 weeks or 7 months) (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS).
[CLINICAL] Primary Clinical Outcome (Incontinence Severity): Incontinence severity was defined as the total number of monthly wetting episodes, recorded on weekly patient charts (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). Across the study, the PFME program achieved an overall 85.2% reduction in urinary incontinence episodes, with mean monthly wetting episodes dropping from 79.70 ± 65.13 at baseline to 17.94 ± 25.66 at the fourth visit (siu2003.pdf.md, Aim; RESULTS).
[CLINICAL] Secondary Outcomes (Muscle Strength and Technique):
Pelvic Muscle Strength: Assessed digitally by a single nurse specialist using the 6-point modified Oxford scale (grade 0 = no contraction to grade 5 = strong contraction with cranial lift) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). The program achieved an overall 73.1% increase in pelvic muscle strength (siu2003.pdf.md, Aim; RESULTS). Baseline strength averaged 1.67 ± 0.60 among study completers and 1.84 ± 0.57 among dropouts (siu2003.pdf.md, TABLE I).
Exercise Technique Correctness: Evaluated as a binary variable (correct vs. incorrect execution without accessory muscle substitution) (siu2003.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). The proportion of women performing incorrect contractions remained low, measuring 0.9% at initial instruction and 1.9% at the fourth visit (siu2003.pdf.md, RESULTS).
