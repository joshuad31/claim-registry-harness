5. Stated Limits and Transferability to Non-Surgical Voiding Dysfunction
[CLINICAL] Both source documents state explicit population boundaries restricting the transferability of their findings to non-surgical voiding dysfunction.
A. Surgical Cohort Restrictions
Post-Operative Focus: Both studies evaluated men exclusively within the context of recent surgical interventions—TURP for BPH in Xiao et al. (11255_2025_Article_4454.pdf.md, Abstract), and radical prostatectomy or TURP in Glazener et al. (glazener2011.pdf.md, Methods - Participants).
Exclusion of Non-Surgical LUTS / Detrusor Dysfunction:
Glazener et al. explicitly excluded men undergoing channel TURP for advanced prostate cancer and those scheduled for radiotherapy (glazener2011.pdf.md, Methods - Participants).
[NO SOURCE] Neither study included non-surgical men with primary overactive bladder (OAB), detrusor underactivity (DUA), or non-surgical BPH/LUTS (11255_2025_Article_4454.pdf.md, Inclusion and exclusion criteria; glazener2011.pdf.md, Methods - Participants).
B. Specific Author Statements on Generalizability
Xiao et al. (2025): Highlight single-center retrospective limitations: "our sample was drawn from a single institution, which may limit the generalizability of findings to diverse populations with varying healthcare practices and resources" (11255_2025_Article_4454.pdf.md, Discussion).
Glazener et al. (2011): Limit conclusions strictly to post-prostate surgery incontinence in public-information environments: "In settings where information about pelvic-floor exercise is widely available, one-to-one conservative physical therapy for men who are incontinent after prostate surgery is unlikely to be effective or cost effective" (glazener2011.pdf.md, Summary - Interpretation; Discussion). The authors explicitly emphasize that their study did not test whether PFMT is effective for non-surgical voiding dysfunction or in settings where basic continence advice is absent (glazener2011.pdf.md, Discussion).
Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence Type Differentiation:
[CLINICAL]: In a single-center retrospective study (Xiao et al., N=180), HBM-guided PFMT post-TURP significantly reduced 6-month IPSS scores (5.16 vs. 8.39, P<0.001) and overall surgical complications (6.02% vs. 15.46%, P=0.045). Conversely, in two large multicenter RCTs (Glazener et al., MAPS trial, N=853), formal 1-to-1 PFMT starting 6 weeks post-op provided no additional benefit over standard lifestyle advice for 12-month continence rates in men after RP (76% vs. 77%, P=0.64) or TURP (65% vs. 62%, P=0.47).
[MECHANISM]: Postoperative PFMT aims to strengthen the striated external urethral sphincter and levator ani musculature to compensate for surgical disruption of the internal urethral sphincter or dynamic outlet resistance.
[INFERRED]: High baseline public availability of continence information (e.g., in the UK NHS) enables self-directed recovery, rendering formal 1-to-1 physical therapy visits non-cost-effective after prostate surgery compared to standard lifestyle advice.
[NO SOURCE]: Neither trial evaluated non-surgical men with LUTS, detrusor underactivity, or primary neurogenic voiding dysfunction.
Remaining Clinical Gaps:
Whether intensive preoperative PFMT (prehabilitation) or early post-catheter extraction training yields long-term continence benefits in non-surgical male voiding dysfunction remains unproven.
Standardized, objective adherence tracking protocols are needed to establish true dose-response relationships for male pelvic floor rehabilitation.
