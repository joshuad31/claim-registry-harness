[CLINICAL] Nyanyo et al. (2024):
Design & Cohort: Retrospective matched cohort study conducted at an academic pituitary center evaluating 194 patients with Cushing's disease (CD) who achieved surgical remission following transsphenoidal surgery (TSS), compared with 92 frequency-matched patients with nonfunctioning pituitary adenomas (NFPA) (Nyanyo2024_Cushings_Disease_A.md, ABSTRACT; METHODS, Patients; RESULTS, Clinical Characteristics).
Demographics: CD cohort was 88.1% female (171/194) with a mean age of 43.5 ± 13.8 years, mean BMI of 34.5 ± 8.4 kg/m², and mean adenoma diameter of 5.7 ± 5.4 mm (Nyanyo2024_Cushings_Disease_A.md, Table 1). NFPA control cohort was 84.8% female (78/92) with a mean age of 45.7 ± 13.7 years, mean BMI of 29.5 ± 7.7 kg/m², and mean adenoma diameter of 21.3 ± 7.3 mm (Nyanyo2024_Cushings_Disease_A.md, Table 1).
Follow-up: Mean post-operative follow-up was 68.9 ± 51.0 months in the CD cohort versus 74.6 ± 61.7 months in the NFPA cohort (standardized mean difference [SMD] = -0.12) (Nyanyo2024_Cushings_Disease_A.md, Table 1). Primary cumulative incidence was evaluated at 3 years (36 months) post-surgery (Nyanyo2024_Cushings_Disease_A.md, ABSTRACT).
[CLINICAL] Takasu et al. (1990):
Design & Cohort: Prospective clinical series evaluating 24 consecutive patients (16 women, 8 men; mean age 38 ± 12 years) presenting with Cushing's syndrome due to a hyperfunctioning adrenocortical adenoma who underwent unilateral adrenalectomy (Takasu1990_Cushings_Syndrome_Due.md, Abstract; METHODS, Subjects).
Follow-up: Patients underwent serial endocrine, serological, and thyroid ultrasound evaluations prior to surgery and at regular intervals for up to 24 months post-adrenalectomy (Takasu1990_Cushings_Syndrome_Due.md, RESULTS; Figure 1; Figure 2).

Synthesis and Key Implications
Evidence-Type Integration: All data from the three sources represent human clinical cohort and case-control findings ([CLINICAL]). Hypothesized cellular mechanisms—such as glucocorticoid withdrawal releasing tonic inhibition on T-cell proliferation and cytokine synthesis, unmasking auto-reactive lymphocytes, and 11β-HSD1 hyperactivation driving omental adipocyte differentiation ([MECHANISM])—explain observed post-remission autoimmunity and metaflammation.
Bi-Phasic Immunological Rebound vs. Imprinting: Resolution of hypercortisolism induces an acute rebound in adaptive immunity, increasing the 3-year hazard of overt autoimmune disease by 7.8-fold (Nyanyo2024). Concurrently, innate metabolic inflammation (sTNF-R1, IL-6, central adiposity) remains permanently imprinted for over a decade post-cure (Barahona2009).
Clinical Monitoring and Extrapolation Limits: Clinicians treating Cushing's syndrome must screen for pre-existing autoantibodies and family history to anticipate post-cure autoimmune flares. However, urological researchers must recognize that endocrine hypercortisolism metrics cannot substitute for direct urodynamic evaluation when defining voiding dysfunction cohorts.
