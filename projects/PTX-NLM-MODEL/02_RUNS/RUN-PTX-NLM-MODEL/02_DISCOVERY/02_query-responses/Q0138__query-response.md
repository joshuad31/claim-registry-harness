A. Akin et al. (2010) (nihms496647.pdf.md)
[CLINICAL] Proposed Diagnostic Framework: Establishes a 4-part diagnostic requirement for MCAS (nihms496647.pdf.md, Proposed Criteria for the diagnosis of idiopathic mast cell activation syndrome; Table II):
Multisystem Clinical Manifestations: Recurrent episodic symptoms affecting ≥2 organ systems (Skin: urticaria, angioedema, flushing; GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramping; CV: hypotensive syncope/near syncope, tachycardia; Respiratory: wheezing; Naso-ocular: conjunctival injection, pruritus, nasal stuffiness) (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II).
Therapeutic Response: Resolution or significant reduction of symptoms with anti-mediator therapy (H1/H2 histamine receptor antagonists, antileukotrienes, or cromolyn sodium) (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II).
Biochemical Proof: Documented elevation of a validated serum or urinary marker above baseline during a symptomatic period on ≥2 occasions (or 1 occasion if baseline tryptase is persistently > 15 ng/mL) (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II).
Exclusion: Ruling out primary clonal mast cell disease (systemic mastocytosis [SM] or monoclonal MCAS [MMAS]) and secondary causes of MC activation (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II).
B. Gülen (2024) (Gulen2024_MCAS.md)
[CLINICAL] Vienna Consensus Criteria: Enforces 3 compulsory criteria that must all be satisfied to confirm an MCAS diagnosis (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS; Fig. 1):
Clinical Criterion: Typical, severe, episodic MC activation symptoms simultaneously involving ≥2 organ systems, typically meeting the clinical criteria for anaphylaxis (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS; Fig. 1, Fig. 2).
Laboratory Criterion: Substantial transient increase in a validated marker of systemic MC activation during an acute episode compared with individual baseline, specifically fulfilling the formula Event Tryptase>(sBT×1.2+2) ng/mL (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS).
Response Criterion: Control of clinical symptoms using MC mediator-targeting pharmacotherapy (H1/H2 blockers, leukotriene antagonists, MC stabilizers, or aspirin/NSAIDs) (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS).

A. Points of Consensus
[INFERRED] Core Triad: Both frameworks agree that MCAS requires severe, episodic multisystem symptoms (≥2 organ systems), objective transient biomarker elevation over individual baseline, and anti-mediator treatment response (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II; Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS).
[INFERRED] Exclusion Requirement: Both demand rigorous exclusion of primary clonal mast cell disease (SM, MMAS) via bone marrow staging and secondary allergic/inflammatory etiologies (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table I; Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Primary ‘‘Clonal’’ MCAS).
B. Points of Divergence
[INFERRED] Mathematical Precision of Tryptase Elevation: Akin et al. (2010) accept any elevation above baseline on ≥2 occasions without a fixed threshold (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II). Gülen (2024) enforces the strict consensus formula >1.2×sBT+2 ng/mL to eliminate background noise and non-MC fluctuations (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS).
[INFERRED] Symptom Threshold & Overdiagnosis Prevention: Akin et al. (2010) include broader naso-ocular symptoms (pruritus, stuffiness) (nihms496647.pdf.md, Table II). Gülen (2024) explicitly restricts clinical criteria to severe episodic attacks meeting anaphylaxis standards, rejecting chronic non-specific complaints (fibromyalgia, IBS, POTS, fatigue) as MCAS criteria to prevent misdiagnosis (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, Diagnosis of MCAS; Fig. 4).
[INFERRED] Clonality Screening Algorithm: Gülen (2024) updates the diagnostic algorithm to include peripheral blood KIT D816V mutation testing and HαT screening (for sBT ≥8 ng/mL) before proceeding to invasive bone marrow biopsy (Gulen2024_MCAS.md, When a Bone Marrow Investigation Should Be Performed; Fig. 3).
