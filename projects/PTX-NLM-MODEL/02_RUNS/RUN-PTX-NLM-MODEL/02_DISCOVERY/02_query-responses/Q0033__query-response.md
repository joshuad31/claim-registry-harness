Cornel et al. (2005) (prospective cohort, N=31 men with CP/CPPS): Evaluated s-EMG rectal probe biofeedback training and instruction in pelvic floor muscle relaxation to decrease elevated resting tone (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, Table 2).

4. Reporting and Adherence Limitations Identified by the Reviews
[CLINICAL] van Reijn-Baggen et al. (2022) (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, ABSTRACT & RESULTS):
Methodological Quality & Bias: 6 of the 10 included studies were rated as low quality and 4 as medium quality (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, ABSTRACT). The primary methodological limitation was the lack of control groups in 60% of studies (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, ABSTRACT & RESULTS).
Adherence & Exposure Reporting Deficits: The review noted that adherence to home exercise protocols was not systematically measured or reported in most male cohorts (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, RESULTS). Furthermore, treatment protocols lacked standardization regarding session frequency, biofeedback parameters, and duration, preventing quantitative meta-analysis (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, ABSTRACT & RESULTS).
Durability Gaps: Long-term follow-up beyond immediate post-treatment was absent in most male studies, leaving the durability of muscle relaxation and voiding improvement unconfirmed (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
[CLINICAL] Hall et al. (2018) (hall2018.pdf.md, Abstract & 4.1 | Heterogeneity in the reporting of exercise interventions):
Suboptimal Exercise Reporting (CERT Evaluation): Application of adapted Consensus on Exercise Reporting Template (CERT) criteria revealed widespread omissions of critical exercise details across the 108 male studies (hall2018.pdf.md, Abstract & 4.1 | Heterogeneity in the reporting of exercise interventions):
Omission of Home Exercise Prescription: 47 studies failed to report the number of contractions per session, 45 omitted sessions per day, and 43 omitted total daily contractions (hall2018.pdf.md, 3.2 | HEP details).
Unconfirmed Contraction Execution: 25 studies (23%) contained no description of how, or if, the pelvic floor muscle contraction was confirmed or assessed to ensure correct execution (hall2018.pdf.md, 3.1 | PFMT session details).
Adherence Measurement Gaps: Adherence to the home exercise program was completely unmeasured or unreported in the vast majority of manuscripts (hall2018.pdf.md, Abstract & TABLE 4). Where measured, methods were inconsistent (e.g., self-report vs. paper logbook vs. electronic download) (hall2018.pdf.md, TABLE 4).
Absence of Progression & Motivation Rules: Most studies omitted decision rules for exercise progression (e.g., advancing hold times or resistance) and failed to describe motivation strategies to maintain compliance (hall2018.pdf.md, Abstract & 107 | Item 5–7).

Synthesis and Key Implications
[CLINICAL] Divergence in Therapeutic Objectives: The two systematic reviews summarize fundamentally opposing pelvic floor physical therapy paradigms in male urology (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, ABSTRACT; hall2018.pdf.md, 1 | INTRODUCTION). While treatment for post-prostatectomy incontinence and lower urinary tract symptoms focuses on high-intensity muscular strengthening and urethral closure enhancement (hall2018.pdf.md), management of chronic pelvic pain and hypertonicity relies on biofeedback-assisted down-training, muscle relaxation, and myofascial release (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md).
[MECHANISM] Risk of Misapplied Strengthening: Inappropriate prescription of high-intensity contraction/strengthening exercises (Kegel exercises) to men with pelvic floor hypertonicity or detrusor-sphincter pseudodyssynergia can exacerbate muscle spasm, elevate intravesical voiding pressures, and worsen pelvic pain (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, INTRODUCTION & DISCUSSION).
[INFERRED] Critical Methodological & Reporting Barriers:
Paucity of Standardized Adherence Measurement: Both reviews establish that adherence monitoring is neglected in male pelvic physical therapy literature, preventing clinicians from distinguishing treatment non-response from non-compliance (vanreijn-baggen2022.pdf.md, RESULTS; hall2018.pdf.md, 4.1).
Need for CERT Compliance: Future clinical trials in male pelvic floor rehabilitation must adopt standardized reporting frameworks (such as CERT) to explicitly detail contraction targets, confirmation methods, exercise progression rules, and verified adherence metrics (hall2018.pdf.md, 4.1 & 5 | CONCLUSION).
