A. Myers et al. (1995) (myers1995.pdf.md)
Species & Model: [PRECLINICAL] Adult male Sprague-Dawley rats weighing 350 g (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Surgical model). Splanchnic ischemia was induced by temporary occlusion of the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) and collateral vessels for 20 minutes, followed by 30 minutes of reperfusion (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Preparation of the rat for mesenteric ischemia/reperfusion injury). Following in vivo reperfusion, the right kidney was harvested and perfused ex vivo with oxygenated Krebs–Henseleit buffer at 5 mL/min (maintaining 100 mmHg perfusion pressure) to measure de novo renal eicosanoid release into venous effluent (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Preparation of the ex vivo isolated renal perfusion).
Dose, Route, and Timing: Parent PTX was administered intravenously (IV) at a dose of 50 mg/kg (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Surgical model). Administration was split: 10 minutes prior to SMA occlusion and 1 minute prior to reperfusion (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Surgical model).
Comparative Control Groups: Evaluated against non-ischemic sham controls (n=9), untreated ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) vehicle controls (n=6), and enteral allopurinol controls (10 mg/kg/day orally for 5 days prior to ischemia, n=8) (myers1995.pdf.md, METHODS - Surgical model).
B. Refsum et al. (1996) (refsum1996.pdf.md)
Species & Model: [PRECLINICAL] Female Wistar rats weighing 180 to 240 g (refsum1996.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS). Sublethal peritonitis and polymicrobial sepsis were induced by cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) using a 21-gauge needle (refsum1996.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS - Group 3).
Dose, Route, and Timing: Parent PTX was administered as a single intraperitoneal (IP) injection at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight (refsum1996.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS - Group 4). Administration occurred 1 hour after the CLP procedure (refsum1996.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS - Group 4).
Comparative Control Groups: Evaluated across 4 randomized cohorts (n=8 per time point at 6, 12, 18, and 24 hours): Group 1 (Time 0 normal controls, n=8), Group 2 (Sham laparotomy with cecal handling, n=32), Group 3 (CLP alone, n=32), and Group 4 (CLP + PTX 20 mg/kg IP at 1 h, n=32) (refsum1996.pdf.md, MATERIALS AND METHODS).

B. Detailed Cytokine Dynamics in Refsum et al. (1996)
[PRECLINICAL] [MECHANISM] Bioactive plasma cytokines exhibited distinct temporal profiles:
TNF-α: Unchanged during the first 18 hours post-CLP (baseline ∼5 pg/mL), but surged at 24 hours in untreated CLP rats (18.54±5.49 pg/mL) (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS - TNF α). IP PTX (20 mg/kg) significantly suppressed this 24-hour TNF-α peak to 6.48±2.00 pg/mL (p<0.001) (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS - TNF α).
IL-6: Displayed a biphasic pattern. The initial 6-hour peak (∼2000 pg/mL) reflected surgical trauma and was uninfluenced by PTX (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS - IL-6; DISCUSSION). The second 24-hour peak in untreated CLP rats (1671±409 pg/mL) was significantly blunted by PTX (648±87 pg/mL, p<0.01), demonstrating that PTX selectively targets TNF-α-driven septic cytokine amplification (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS - IL-6; DISCUSSION).
Hemodynamics & Survival: Both sham and CLP rats survived 24 hours (100% survival) (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS). Blood pressure dropped significantly at 24 hours in all groups (∼79–83 mmHg, p<0.05), and PTX did not prevent this hypotensive decline, indicating that late hypotension in sublethal peritonitis is driven by non-TNF-α pathways (refsum1996.pdf.md, RESULTS - Blood Pressure; DISCUSSION).
