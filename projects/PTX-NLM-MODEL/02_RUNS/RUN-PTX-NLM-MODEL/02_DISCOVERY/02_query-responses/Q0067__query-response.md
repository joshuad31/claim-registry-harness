Study Design, Patient Cohorts, and Matching Protocols
[CLINICAL] In Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, the authors conducted a monocentric, retrospective observational study utilizing prospectively collected data from the German Cushing's Registry (LMU Munich, 2012–2021) to characterize the clinical and immunometabolic dynamics of the postoperative glucocorticoid withdrawal phase (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Methods > Patients).
[CLINICAL] The evaluation incorporated a two-step analytical design (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Methods > Patients):
Main Longitudinal Cohort (N=80): Included 80 adult patients with overt endogenous Cushing's syndrome (CS) who achieved surgical disease remission (60 females [75%], 20 males [25%]; median age 43 years [interquartile range, IQR: 33; 54]; 55 pituitary CS [69%], 21 adrenal CS [26%], 4 ectopic CS [5%]) (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Methods > Patients; Table 1). Postoperative assessments were conducted at baseline (active CS prior to surgery) and at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 months after curative surgery (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Methods > Patients). At 1 month post-op, 71 of 80 patients (89%) exhibited secondary adrenal insufficiency and received hydrocortisone replacement (initial dose 40–60 mg/day, tapered to 20–25 mg/day by 3 months) (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Methods > Patients).
Matched Case-Control Subgroup (N=25): A subgroup of 25 CS patients with persistent adrenal insufficiency for ≥1 year was matched 1:1 with 25 control subjects (evaluated for but ruled out for CS) based on age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Methods > Patients; Results > Remission phase after surgery for CS is characterized by a state of low-grade inflammation).

Authors' Conclusions: Predictive Value vs. Biological Causation
[CLINICAL] The authors conclude that early postoperative C-reactive protein and Interleukin-6 elevations serve as quantitative predictive biomarkers / surrogate indicators for persistent glucocorticoid withdrawal symptoms, identifying patients at risk for prolonged myopathy and delayed functional muscle recovery 1 year after surgery (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Significance; Discussion; Conclusion).
[INFERRED] The authors explicitly distinguish statistical prediction from biological causation (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion > Limitations, strengths, and outlook):
Observational Limit: The study design is observational and retrospective, establishing correlations and associations rather than direct causation (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion > Limitations, strengths, and outlook). The authors note: "it is unclear whether this is an association or causally related given that the molecular processes underlying glucocorticoid-induced myopathy are poorly understood" (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion).
Metaflammation & Rebound Immunity Model: The authors hypothesize that surgical removal of excess cortisol relieves chronic immunosuppression, triggering a transient "rebound immunity" or "metaflammation" state (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Discussion). This inflammatory surge is particularly pronounced in patients with persistent obesity (BMI>30 kg m
−2
) and hyperglycemia (elevated HbA1c and HOMA-IR) due to macrophage infiltration in dysfunctional adipose tissue (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Significance; Discussion).
Clinical Unanswered Question: Whether actively suppressing early post-op inflammation (e.g., via biomarker-guided hydrocortisone dose adjustments) directly causes improved muscle recovery remains unproven and requires prospective randomized controlled trials (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion).

Synthesis and Key Implications
[MECHANISM] The findings in Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md establish that the postoperative glucocorticoid withdrawal phase following cure of Cushing's syndrome is characterized by a 1-year low-grade systemic inflammatory state (CRP and IL-6 elevation), driven by metabolic risk factors (obesity, hyperglycemia) and rebound immunity (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Discussion).
[INFERRED] Critical clinical boundaries emerge from this study:
Biomarker Utility: Early postoperative CRP and IL-6 levels (1 to 3 months) provide objective predictive surrogate markers to identify patients at risk for delayed muscle function recovery and myopathy 1 year after surgery (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Abstract; Significance; Discussion).
Observational Bounds: These correlations do not prove that inflammation directly causes muscle weakness, nor do they prove that anti-inflammatory interventions will restore muscle strength ([NO SOURCE], Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion). Randomized interventional trials are required before altering postoperative hydrocortisone substitution protocols (Vogel2023_Glucocorticoid_Withdrawal.md, Discussion).
