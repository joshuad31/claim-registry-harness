[CLINICAL] Study Design: Open-label, single-arm prospective clinical trial involving 12 patients with systemic sclerosis (SSc; 10 women, 2 men; mean age 43.6±17.8 years; 9 diffuse, 3 limited cutaneous subtype) (desouza2009.pdf.md, Abstract; Patients and methods > Study design; Results > Clinical features of the treated group).

[CLINICAL] Duration: 24 weeks (6 months) of continuous daily administration, evaluated at 4-week intervals (desouza2009.pdf.md, Abstract; Patients and methods > Study design).

2. Measured Fibrosis Outcomes and Statistics
[CLINICAL] Primary Endpoint (Modified Rodnan Skin Score, MRSS):
Treated Combination Group (n=12): Mean baseline MRSS of 25.70±6.74 decreased to 18.79±8.62 at week 16 (P=0.03), 17.58±9.68 at week 20 (P=0.02), and 17.29±9.93 at week 24 (P=0.02 vs. baseline; overall baseline to 24-week endpoint P=0.003, Wilcoxon signed-rank test) (desouza2009.pdf.md, Results > Primary endpoint; Table 1). Good Response (>50% MRSS reduction): 25% (3 of 12 patients) (desouza2009.pdf.md, Results > Secondary endpoints > MRSS).
Moderate Response (25%–50% MRSS reduction): 50% (6 of 12 patients) (desouza2009.pdf.md, Results > Secondary endpoints > MRSS).

3. Separation Capabilities of the Trial Design
[INFERRED] What the Design CAN Separate:
The single-arm intervention design can demonstrate the overall clinical efficacy of the combined PTX + vitamin E regimen against pre-treatment baseline self-controls (P=0.003) and shows superior skin score reduction compared to a historical cyclophosphamide control cohort (P=0.003 vs. P=0.06) (desouza2009.pdf.md, Abstract; Results > Primary endpoint; Discussion).
[INFERRED] What the Design CANNOT Separate:
The trial design cannot isolate or separate the specific therapeutic contribution of pentoxifylline alone versus vitamin E alone, nor can it establish whether true drug-drug synergy occurred, because all treated participants received both agents simultaneously without single-agent monotherapy arms or placebo controls (desouza2009.pdf.md, Patients and methods > Study design; Discussion).

[CLINICAL] Study Design: Monocentric, double-blind, 2×2 factorial randomized placebo-controlled trial in 24 eligible women (mean age 57±8 years) with 29 superficial radiation-induced fibrosis (RIF) areas following breast cancer radiotherapy (22 patients with 27 RIF areas fully evaluateable at 6 months) (delanian2003.pdf.md, Abstract; PATIENTS AND METHODS > Randomization; RESULTS; Table 1).
[CLINICAL] Doses & Route: Oral parent pentoxifylline 800 mg/day (400 mg bid) and/or oral alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) 1,000 U/day (500 U bid) (delanian2003.pdf.md, Abstract; PATIENTS AND METHODS > Randomization).
[CLINICAL] Duration: 6 months of continuous daily treatment (delanian2003.pdf.md, Abstract; PATIENTS AND METHODS > Randomization).
[CLINICAL] Four Factorial Treatment Arms:
Group A (PTX + Vit E, n=6 patients / 7 RIF areas allocated; n=5 patients / 6 areas analyzed at 6 mo): Active PTX 800 mg/d + Active Vit E 1,000 U/d (delanian2003.pdf.md, PATIENTS AND METHODS > Randomization; Table 1; Table 3).

3. Separation Capabilities of the Trial Design
[INFERRED] What the Design CAN Separate:
The 2×2 factorial randomized controlled design can rigorously separate and isolate the individual therapeutic effects of pentoxifylline monotherapy (Group B vs. D), vitamin E monotherapy (Group C vs. D), and their combination (Group A vs. B, C, and D) (delanian2003.pdf.md, PATIENTS AND METHODS > Randomization; DISCUSSION).

[MECHANISM] Both studies demonstrate that parent pentoxifylline combined with alpha-tocopherol (vitamin E) modifies pathological extracellular matrix remodeling by downregulating transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) signaling, reducing fibroblast collagen synthesis, scavenging reactive oxygen species, and improving tissue microperfusion (desouza2009.pdf.md, Introduction; Discussion; delanian2003.pdf.md, DISCUSSION).
[INFERRED] Methodological comparisons yield crucial clinical insights:
Monotherapy Inefficacy vs. Combination Synergy: As conclusively established by the factorial design of delanian2003.pdf.md, pentoxifylline monotherapy or vitamin E monotherapy fails to halt or reverse established tissue fibrosis (performing no better than placebo), whereas their combined administration exerts synergistic antifibrotic activity (delanian2003.pdf.md, Abstract; DISCUSSION).
Design Limitations: Single-arm combination trials (desouza2009.pdf.md) confirm open-label clinical utility in systemic sclerosis but cannot prove whether pentoxifylline or vitamin E is the active driver (desouza2009.pdf.md, Patients and methods > Study design; Discussion). Factorial RCTs (delanian2003.pdf.md) remain the gold standard for dissecting dual-drug antifibrotic regimens.
