[MECHANISM]: PTX elevates intracellular cAMP via non-specific PDE inhibition, improving erythrocyte deformability and microvascular perfusion. However, high doses or delayed administration cause uncontrolled peripheral arterial vasodilation, precipitating cellular hypoxia and severe lactic acidosis.
[INFERRED]: A narrow "therapeutic window" governs PTX in sepsis: early administration during adequate volume resuscitation provides microvascular protection, but delayed dosing in established shock or excessive rapid dosing during trauma resuscitation precipitates fatal vasodilatory collapse.
[NO SOURCE]: None of these animal studies measured active human oxidative metabolites (M1, M4, M5) or evaluated human clinical cohorts.
Key Implications for Practice:
PTX cannot be safely administered to unresuscitated patients in established septic shock or major hypovolemic trauma without risking catastrophic arterial hypotension.
Clinical translational protocols must utilize low, controlled infusion rates (e.g. 1.0–1.5 mg/kg/h) combined with aggressive volume expansion to capture microvascular protective benefits while avoiding vasodilatory toxicity.
