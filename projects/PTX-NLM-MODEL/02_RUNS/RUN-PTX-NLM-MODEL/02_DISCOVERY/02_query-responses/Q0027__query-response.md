Synthesis and Key Implications
[CLINICAL] Evidence Synthesis: Epidemiological estimates for UAB and DUA vary widely (from 12% to 48% in elderly men and 12% to 24% in elderly women) because studies rely on heterogeneous urodynamic definitions across selected clinic series rather than population-based sampling (2020ICU_icu-58-S68.pdf.md, Abstract; PREVALENCE AND EPIDEMIOLOGY OF DETRUSOR UNDERACTIVITY) (chung2014.pdf.md, Introduction). DUA prevalence is strongly age-dependent and reaches its highest rates in institutionalized elderly patients (66.7%) and diabetic men with LUTS (79%) (2020ICU_icu-58-S68.pdf.md, PREVALENCE AND EPIDEMIOLOGY OF DETRUSOR UNDERACTIVITY).
[INFERRED] Methodological Limits & Gaps: Because current estimates rely entirely on invasive urodynamics in patients already seeking care for bothersome LUTS, these figures cannot be extrapolated directly to asymptomatic general populations (2020ICU_icu-58-S68.pdf.md, Abstract; PREVALENCE AND EPIDEMIOLOGY OF DETRUSOR UNDERACTIVITY). Establishing non-invasive, validated symptom questionnaires (such as the UAB-Q) and standardized clinical diagnostic algorithms will be necessary to obtain true population-based prevalence data for UAB and related psychogenic voiding conditions like paruresis.
