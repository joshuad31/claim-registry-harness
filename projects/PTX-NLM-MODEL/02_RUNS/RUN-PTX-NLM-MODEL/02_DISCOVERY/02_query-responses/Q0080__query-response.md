Synthesis and Key Implications
[INFERRED] Plausible Design Differences Explaining Divergent Results:
Fundamental Pathophysiology & Target Indication (Post-Surgical Incontinence vs. Non-Surgical BPH/OAB Storage Dysfunction):
Glazener et al. (2011) evaluated post-surgical urinary incontinence (sphincteric incompetence / intraoperative denervation 6 weeks post-RP or post-TURP), where acute anatomical disruption dominates and spontaneous natural recovery occurs over 12 months in both intervention and control groups (glazener2011.pdf.md, Introduction & Discussion).
Hagovska & Svihra (2024) evaluated non-surgical male storage symptoms (OAB and BPH with persistent detrusor overactivity/urgency), where structural bladder outlet resistance and detrusor overactivity respond directly to behavioral urgency suppression techniques and reflex detrusor inhibition via active pelvic floor contraction (s00345-024-04974-7.pdf.md, Abstract & Introduction).
Intervention Supervision & Total Therapeutic Contact Dose:
Glazener et al. (2011) provided a low-intensity intervention consisting of only 4 therapist sessions spaced over 12 weeks (glazener2011.pdf.md, Summary & Discussion).
Hagovska & Svihra (2024) delivered a high-intensity intervention comprising 12 weekly 1-on-1 supervised sessions (30 min/day, 5 days/week home practice) incorporating explicit urgency suppression techniques (PFMT-st), establishing a 3-fold higher direct supervision exposure (s00345-024-04974-7.pdf.md, Experimental and control groups & Table 2).
Control Group Contamination & Baseline Exercise Exposure:
In Glazener et al. (2011), standard care controls in the UK NHS had widespread access to PFMT information; 84% of RP controls and 21% of TURP controls were already performing pelvic floor exercises at baseline, and 50% (RP) and 20% (TURP) were still doing PFMT at 12 months, creating substantial control arm crossover/contamination (glazener2011.pdf.md, Table 1, Table 3, & Discussion).
In Hagovska & Svihra (2024), control participants received pharmacological monotherapy (silodosin 8 mg) without physiotherapist access, physical instructions, or urgency suppression training (s00345-024-04974-7.pdf.md, Patients and methods / Allocation).
Synergy with Pharmacotherapy:
Glazener et al. (2011) evaluated physical therapy alone without standardized concomitant medication (glazener2011.pdf.md, Methods).
