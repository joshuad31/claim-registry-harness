[CLINICAL]: Unilateral adrenalectomy for Cushing's adenoma triggers overt autoimmune thyroid dysfunction in 12.5% (3/24) of patients, manifesting as transient silent thyroiditis (Patient 1), permanent primary hypothyroidism (Patient 2), or biphasic thyroiditis transitioning to hypothyroidism (Patient 3).

[INFERRED]: Screening all Cushing's syndrome patients for antithyroid autoantibodies preoperatively allows clinicians to identify high-risk individuals (seropositive rate 21%) who require long-term thyroid function monitoring postoperatively.
[NO SOURCE]: Takasu et al. did not measure circulating inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) or evaluate pentoxifylline co-administration.
Key Implications for Practice:
Preoperative adrenal steroid levels do not predict postoperative thyroid complications, but baseline antithyroid antibody positivity is highly predictive (60% risk in antibody-positive cases).
Patients cured of Cushing's syndrome who carry antithyroid antibodies should be monitored with serial TSH and thyroid hormone panels for at least 2 years post-adrenalectomy.
