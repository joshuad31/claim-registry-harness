# Foliar silicate and wheat yield (WORKED EXAMPLE — synthetic)

> Fabricated for testing the harness. Not research. Do not cite.

## 1. Mission

Test whether a foliar silicate treatment increases grain yield of drought-stressed
wheat in field trials.

## 2. Frozen hypotheses

### axis one
Does foliar silicate increase grain yield of drought-stressed wheat in field trials?

**Governing boundary.** H1 requires field evidence at a stated rate. H2 requires
field evidence that the effect is absent. H3 applies when the field evidence is
mixed.

**Cohort:** Wheat grown in field trials under drought.

| Hypothesis | Human-written statement |
|---|---|
| **A1-H1** | Foliar silicate increases grain yield of drought-stressed wheat in field trials. The increase is reported at a foliar rate of two millilitres per litre. The yield increase is larger when drought is severe. |
| **A1-H2** | Foliar silicate does not increase grain yield of drought-stressed wheat in field trials. Field trials at a foliar rate of two millilitres per litre report no yield difference. |
| **A1-H3** | Existing field evidence cannot determine whether foliar silicate increases grain yield of drought-stressed wheat under drought. The field findings are inconsistent across trials. |

#### A1-H1 — Foliar silicate raises field yield under drought

**Assumptions and antecedents:** The foliar rate is two millilitres per litre and the trials are conducted under drought.

**Potential falsification:** Field trials at that rate report no yield difference.

**Potential corroboration:** Field trials at that rate report a larger yield increase when drought is severe.

#### A1-H2 — The field yield effect is absent

**Assumptions and antecedents:** The null trials used the same foliar rate of two millilitres per litre.

**Potential falsification:** A field trial at that rate reports a yield increase.

**Potential corroboration:** Multi-site field trials report no yield difference at that rate.

#### A1-H3 — The field evidence is inconsistent

**Assumptions and antecedents:** Field trials at a comparable rate have been retrieved.

**Potential falsification:** Consistent field trials establish either a yield benefit or its absence at that rate.

**Potential corroboration:** Field findings differ across trials without a clear rate or timing explanation.
