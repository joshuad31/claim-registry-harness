**Core unit of analysis:** One proposed causal link in the chain from clinically attainable PTX or metabolite exposure, through a relevant biological target and tissue effect, to changed voiding function, an identifiable patient subgroup, and a measurable trial prediction. Every query must strengthen, break, or localize uncertainty in at least one such link.

**H1 success standard:** H1 does not mean that PTX is proven effective, proven safe, or ready to prescribe. H1 wins if the existing evidence makes a targeted clinical trial scientifically compelling and specific enough to design. **Missing causal links do not defeat H1 where the supported premises and their discriminating predictions are themselves sufficient to design the trial that would resolve those links.** A trial's purpose is to resolve residual mechanistic uncertainty; residual uncertainty is therefore not by itself a reason the trial is unjustified.

**The trial-specification test.** This is the operational discriminator between H1 and H3. Ask whether the surviving evidence can specify all four:

- **(a) Intervention and exposure** — agent, any required cointervention, route, dose, and duration at a clinically attainable exposure.
- **(b) Phenotype** — measurable baseline characteristics identifying the subgroup proposed to respond, ascertainable before treatment.
- **(c) Measurement** — the primary functional outcome, its expected direction, and its expected timing.
- **(d) Comparator** — the control or contrast that would distinguish the proposed mechanism from the leading alternative explanation.

Filling all four slots is necessary but not sufficient for H1: the specification must rest on materially supported premises, explicit transfer assumptions, discriminating predictions, and serious adverse testing. An affirmative finding that defeats the materially supported rationale is H2-type. A named unresolved issue that prevents a defensible trial rationale is H3-type; the ledger must identify the unresolved link and the slot it blocks. Uncertainties that the proposed trial can legitimately test do not by themselves favor H3.

## 2. Frozen hypotheses

### **axis one:** 
Does the existing mechanistic evidence make a compelling case that PTX can address an untreated cause of impaired voiding in this cohort—and therefore warrants a targeted clinical trial?

**Governing boundary.** H1 requires sufficient mechanistic grounding to justify a specific, informative clinical trial—not completion of every causal link. H2 requires affirmative evidence against the materially supported therapeutic rationale. H3 applies when the unresolved links prevent that rationale from becoming specific and defensible enough to design that trial. The three are mutually exclusive under the trial-specification test in §1; if a finding appears to support two of them, the test has not been applied.

**Cohort:** Men in the United States identified as having BPH, shy bladder, and difficulty voiding, with inadequate benefit from tamsulosin **and** prior prescribed pelvic-floor treatment failure or abandonment because it seemed ineffective. Preserve those two pelvic-floor histories separately. Give rapid improvement and slower recovery equal investigative priority.

#### A1-H1 — PTX addresses a consequential, treatable mechanism that existing interventions leave unresolved

**Assumptions and antecedents:** The relevant dysfunction occurs in this cohort; the proposed drug effects reach the relevant tissue under plausible treatment conditions; and changing those processes can improve urinary function. Any necessary contribution from vitamin E or another cointervention is explicit.

**Potential falsification:** Relevant exposure is unattainable; the proposed target is absent or incidental; target modulation fails to improve the relevant function under informative conditions; or stronger evidence breaks a link necessary to every route that is not speculative.

**Potential corroboration:** Converging findings connect attainable exposure, target engagement, physiological improvement, and relevant function. Timing, dose-response relationships, response predictors, and contrary results discriminate this explanation from alternatives. The evidence identifies measurable patient characteristics and trial outcomes that should reveal benefit. **H1 is supported when the four trial-specification elements form a coherent, source-grounded test of a materially supported therapeutic rationale that survives serious adverse testing. Merely filling the elements is insufficient; unresolved links are permissible when the proposed experiment can discriminate them.**

#### A1-H2 — The proposed therapeutic mechanism fails at a necessary causal link

**Assumptions and antecedents:** The evidence demonstrating the mismatch is sufficiently relevant to test H1; failure in an unrelated model or exposure does not suffice. H2 targets the materially supported rationale as it currently stands in the ledger, not every route that could be imagined.

**Potential falsification:** A coherent, exposure-compatible causal chain survives the proposed objections and generates supported functional predictions in relevant conditions.

**Potential corroboration:** Relevant experiments demonstrate insufficient exposure, absent target engagement, persistent dysfunction despite target modulation, incompatible timing, or another affirmative failure of a necessary link. **Missing clinical trials alone do not corroborate H2.** Breaking one route among several independently supported routes damages that route in proportion to the share of the case it carried; it does not by itself corroborate H2.

#### A1-H3 — The evidence leaves the decisive mechanistic link unresolved

**Assumptions and antecedents:** Meaningful retrieval and adverse testing have been completed; uncertainty is not merely an artifact of an unfinished investigation.

**Potential falsification:** Evidence resolves the decisive links sufficiently to fill all four trial-specification slots (favoring H1) or to defeat the supported rationale (favoring H2).

**Potential corroboration:** Plausible competing explanations remain compatible with the observations because relevant exposure, tissue effects, functional consequences, or response predictors are unmeasured or inadequately distinguished, **and the resulting gap blocks at least one of the four specification slots.** The investigation identifies the experiment that would resolve that uncertainty. Record which slot is blocked and by which link.

### **axis two:** 
If PTX can improve voiding in this cohort, is the proposed benefit predominantly a rapid functional effect or a slow structural recovery?

**Governing boundary.** A2-H1 requires the supported mechanism to predict measurable functional improvement within days to a few weeks at attainable exposure. A2-H2 requires the supported mechanism to act through reversal or limitation of tissue change, predicting improvement only after weeks to months. A2-H3 applies when timing evidence is missing, conflicting, poorly transferable, or consistent with both or neither. The three are mutually exclusive; a finding compatible with both rapid and slow benefit favors A2-H3 unless it shows which effect carries the benefit.

**Relationship to axis one.** Axis two is conditional on a plausible benefit route existing; it does not assess whether a trial is warranted. It informs the expected-timing element of the measurement slot (c) in §1. Do not combine or multiply axis one and axis two assessments. If axis one moves toward H2, axis two should not remain confidently decided.

**Cohort:** Same as axis one.

Its plain-language axis is: A2-H1—the benefit would appear quickly because PTX acts on current function; A2-H2—the benefit would appear slowly because PTX acts on tissue recovery; A2-H3—the existing evidence cannot tell which. | Hypothesis | Test-only statement |
|---|---|
| **A2-H1** | The materially supported mechanism acts on current physiological function—such as perfusion, microcirculation, smooth-muscle behavior, or neural signaling—rather than on established tissue structure. At clinically attainable exposures, PTX or its metabolites should produce measurable improvement in voiding within days to a few weeks, and benefit should diminish if treatment stops. |
| **A2-H2** | The materially supported mechanism acts by reversing or limiting structural change—such as fibrosis, remodeling, or other tissue damage—that contributes to impaired voiding. | **A2-H3** | Existing evidence cannot distinguish rapid functional benefit from slow structural recovery. The uncertainty is localized to identified timing links. |

#### A2-H1 — PTX benefit is a rapid functional effect

**Assumptions and antecedents:** A functional component of impaired voiding is present and modifiable in this cohort; attainable exposure reaches the relevant tissue quickly enough to act within the proposed window.

**Potential falsification:** Relevant models show no early functional change at informative exposures; human benefit, where reported, appears only after prolonged treatment; or the supported route requires structural change before function can improve.

**Potential corroboration:** Functional endpoints such as contractility or flow improve early after exposure; onset tracks dosing; benefit fades on withdrawal; and early change is not explained by concurrent structural recovery.

#### A2-H2 — PTX benefit is a slow structural recovery

**Assumptions and antecedents:** Structural change contributes consequentially to impaired voiding in this cohort, and PTX can reverse or limit that change rather than merely prevent it.

**Potential falsification:** Structural effects appear only when treatment precedes injury (prevention, not reversal); structural improvement does not accompany functional improvement; or meaningful benefit appears too quickly to be structurally mediated.

**Potential corroboration:** Structural endpoints such as stereology or collagen measures improve with treatment of established dysfunction; functional improvement follows structural change in time; benefit accumulates with duration and persists after withdrawal. **Evidence limited to prevention models does not by itself support A2-H2.**

#### A2-H3 — The evidence cannot determine the time course of benefit

**Assumptions and antecedents:** Meaningful retrieval and adverse testing of timing evidence have been completed; uncertainty is not merely an artifact of an unfinished investigation.

**Potential falsification:** Evidence establishes onset, duration-response, or persistence clearly enough to favor A2-H1 or A2-H2.

**Potential corroboration:** Available findings report both functional and structural effects without establishing which carries the benefit, lack onset or withdrawal timing, or rest on models whose timing does not transfer to this cohort. **Record which timing link is unresolved and what measurement would distinguish rapid from slow benefit.**

4. **Choose questions for useful evidence.** Prefer questions likely to uncover a compelling mechanism, expose a potentially fatal objection, resolve a competing explanation, fill or localize a blocked specification slot, or repair source fidelity. **Mechanism-accounting rules:**

- Track each proposed mechanism as its own exposure-to-function chain, and record its **route status**:
  - **Supported** — every necessary link has at least one source-grounded premise at a compatible exposure, tissue, and timescale.
  - **Partial** — one or more necessary links rest on an explicit, stated transfer assumption.
  - **Speculative** — at least one link has no source-grounded premise.
- **Only Supported and Partial routes count as substantive candidate explanations.** A Speculative route is a research idea, not a defence of the trial rationale. Do not substitute a Speculative route when a Supported or Partial route fails.
- Mark every link that is **necessary to more than one route**. Breaking a shared necessary link damages every route that depends on it.
- Affirmative evidence that breaks a link shared by all non-speculative routes or defeats the last Supported/Partial route may defeat the materially supported trial rationale. Failure of only one among several independent surviving routes damages that route without automatically defeating the others; record whether the failed route was central.
- Record affirmative progress only when evidence strengthens a relevant causal link, connects previously supported links, promotes a route's status, or fills a trial-specification slot. A beneficial effect in an unrelated tissue or model is not enough.
- Missing support leaves the affected link unresolved unless retrieval is complete enough to make the absence informative.
- Do not double-count shared sources, repeated pathways, dependent reports, or several effects that all rely on the same unsupported bridge. Two routes sharing a necessary link are not independent evidence merely because they are written as separate routes.

## 3. Decisive-test map

This map guides questions; it is not evidence or a list of predetermined conclusions.

2. **Every query must specify one to three source voices.** Two sources is the ordinary optimum. Use one source when clarification, depth, citation repair, or retrieval repair is the task. Three sources is permitted when genuine comparison or triangulation requires it.
