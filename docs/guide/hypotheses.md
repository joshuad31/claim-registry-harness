# Forming your hypotheses

A hypothesis is one possible answer to your question, worded so that evidence could count for or against it.

You need at least:

- **The answer you think is right.** It is fine to have one, and to say so.
- **A serious rival:** what an informed person who disagrees would say. If nobody really holds it, beating it shows little.
- **"The evidence cannot decide."** A real possibility, not a place to hide.

Three ways in, if you are stuck:

- *Decision first.* What would you do differently if you were right?
- *Hope and objection.* What do you hope is true, and what is the strongest thing a sceptic would say?
- *Bridge.* Are you connecting two fields? Say what each one knows, then the claim that joins them.

Then try the **claim ladder**: write your claim at its strongest, in a middle form, and at the weakest that would still matter. A stronger claim needs more evidence. A narrower one is easier to support and says less.

This is the one place where you may lean toward the answer you want. You do it in the open, and after it the evidence steps cannot be steered.
